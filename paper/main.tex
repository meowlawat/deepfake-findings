\title{Measuring Watermark--Detector Interference: Controls, Score
Attenuation, and Checkpoint Dependence}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Hardik\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}\IEEEauthorrefmark{2} and Yuv Jindal\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}Dept.\ of Computer Science, Indian Institute of Technology Patna, India}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}Dept.\ of Computer Science and Engineering, Vivekananda Institute of Professional Studies -- TC, India}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Prior work reports that embedding a provenance watermark can \emph{harm} a
passive synthetic-image detector inspecting the same pixels. That claim has a
confound: any imperceptible perturbation shifts a detector trained on clean
images, so degradation after marking need not be watermark-specific. We
introduce a control the prior literature does not apply --- a payload-free
perturbation matched per image to the watermark's own PSNR --- and re-measure
the effect on $20{,}000$ held-out images under hypotheses fixed before the
data was scored.

The control reverses the expected conclusion. On an EfficientNet-B6
detector, the degradation attributed to watermarking belongs to the control:
matched-PSNR noise attenuates the detector's log-odds evidence toward zero by
$\approx 16\%$ (slope $0.83$--$0.85$), while watermarking leaves it
essentially intact (slope $0.97$--$0.99$). We then test whether this
attenuation is a property of detection or of one checkpoint, by repeating the
measurement on a second, provenance-disclosed detector matched to the first on
baseline AUC on the same $8{,}000$ images. \textbf{The effect does not
generalise to the second detector tested, and the ordering of the two arms
reverses}, which establishes checkpoint dependence rather than a universal
property of passive detection. Matched baseline AUC closes off a
weak-detector explanation; two detectors do not characterise the population of
passive detectors, and we claim no more than the dependence itself.

What generalises past this dataset is analytic rather than empirical:
monotone attenuation leaves AUC exactly unchanged while still altering the
score-to-posterior mapping that calibration depends on, and a mean-shift
statistic cannot distinguish attenuation from translation where a slope
estimate can. Three supporting measurements are reported as such: both tested
watermark payloads survive a face-replacement-scale splice (bit error rate
$0.0000$ and $\le 0.011$ at $10$--$25\%$ coverage), so neither supplies usable
manipulation evidence at that coverage; attenuation degrades isotonic
calibration without reliably changing decisions under the cost model tested;
and a detector-floor screen found five of six public checkpoints at chance,
one advertising $98.70\%$ accuracy scoring $\mathrm{AUC}=0.54$ in use.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two defences against synthetic media are converging in deployment. Proactive
provenance marking embeds a signal at creation time so origin can be verified
later; passive detection infers manipulation from residual synthesis
artefacts. Both are computed from the same pixels, so they are not
independent: Wu et al.~\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs} report that watermarking
models built for genuine images can \emph{harm} a deployed detector, because
watermark signals overlap the forgery signals detection relies on; the same
pathology is reported independently in audio~\cite{muller2026watermarkshortcut}.
If that holds generally, a detector's score distribution shifting on marked
media invalidates any decision threshold calibrated on unmarked media.

That argument has a load-bearing confound: a detector trained on clean images
responds to \emph{any} imperceptible perturbation, so degradation after
marking need not implicate the watermark's structure. No prior work in this
space separates the two. We do, with a payload-free perturbation matched per
image to the watermark's own PSNR, reporting every interference quantity net
of that arm, with a detector-floor precondition and a gate threshold fixed
before scoring.

Our pre-registered gate returned a null on ranking, and auditing why turned
the result over. The watermark contributed almost nothing; the \emph{control}
carried the effect, and carried it not as a shift but as an attenuation of the
detector's evidence toward zero. A strictly increasing rescaling of scores
leaves AUC exactly unchanged, so a ranking gate is structurally unable to
detect the attenuation component of such a change --- which is why a null on
ranking is consistent with a real change in the score distribution.

That corrected mechanism then failed its own replication. Every number above
came from one third-party checkpoint with undisclosed training data, which
makes ``attenuation is a property of detection'' and ``attenuation is a
property of this model'' observationally identical. We built a second detector
with disclosed provenance, a frozen ImageNet ResNet-18 with a logistic head
fitted only on the training split, and repeated the measurement on images
where the two are matched to within $0.003$ baseline AUC. The ordering did not
hold: on the second detector the payload-free arm attenuates \emph{less} than
the watermarked one (\S\ref{sec:replication}). The attenuation is therefore
checkpoint-dependent across the two models tested, not an established property
of passive detection. Two detectors cannot say which detectors exhibit it;
they can say that a single-checkpoint design, run without a control arm and
read through AUC, would have missed both confounds.

This paper's spine is four results in sequence: a control that overturns an
attribution, the structural reason an AUC-based read misses it, a
replication that bounds how far the mechanism generalises, and what survives
that bounding. Three supporting measurements are reported as such, not as
independent claims of equal weight.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A control that changes the sign of the conclusion.} Every
        interference quantity is reported net of a payload-free perturbation
        matched per image to the watermark's own PSNR. Without it, our own
        headline number would have read as watermark-induced degradation that
        in fact belongs to the control.
  \item \textbf{For this question, a slope is the appropriate estimand.} A
        mean-shift statistic cannot distinguish attenuation from translation,
        so it does not identify which of the two a perturbation produced; the
        per-arm slope does. Separately and structurally, a strictly increasing
        rescaling leaves AUC exactly unchanged (verified by unit test), so a
        ranking-based gate cannot detect a pure attenuation component. We
        claim this for the attenuation-versus-translation question, not that a
        slope is the correct estimand for every interference question.
  \item \textbf{A replication that bounds the claim}
        (\S\ref{sec:replication}). On EfficientNet-B6, the matched-PSNR null
        attenuates evidence by $\approx 16\%$ while watermarking does not; on
        a second, provenance-disclosed detector matched to within $0.003$
        baseline AUC on the same images, the effect does not reproduce and the
        ordering of the two arms reverses. Two detectors establish
        checkpoint-dependence, not which population of detectors exhibits the
        effect, and we claim no more.
  \item \textbf{Supporting measurements}, reported as support rather than as
        further claims. Both tested watermarks retain their payload through a
        face-replacement-scale splice (BER $0.0000$ and $\le 0.011$ at
        $10$--$25\%$ coverage, \S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}); attenuation
        degrades isotonic ECE $4$--$5\times$ without reliably moving decisions
        under the cost model tested (\S\ref{sec:calibration}); and a floor
        screen found five of six public checkpoints at chance, one advertising
        $98.70\%$ accuracy (\S\ref{sec:e0}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Proactive watermarking.} A substantial line of work embeds a mark at
creation time to establish provenance, increasingly unifying provenance with
detection or localisation~\cite{wu2026allinone,zeng2026lava}. Our concern is
orthogonal: what embedding a mark does to a passive detector inspecting the
same pixels, treating watermark strength (one hand-crafted, one learned
scheme) as an experimental axis rather than proposing a new one.

A larger line of work is organised around the tension we measure one instance
of in \S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}: robustness to benign processing and
sensitivity to malicious editing pull in opposite directions, so a mark built
to survive re-encoding is not thereby a good answer to ``was this edited''.
Semi-fragile and separable-decoder designs target localisation explicitly
rather than treating it as a by-product of
robustness~\cite{wu2023sepmark,wang2025fractalforensics,wang2024lampmark,zhang2024editguard,zhang2025omniguard,he2025waveguard,sun2025diffmark,che2026gifguard};
a separate failure surface, diffusion-based regeneration, degrades a payload
globally rather than
locally~\cite{zhang2024zodiac,ni2025diffusionbreaks,qi2026editingaway}. Our
splice measurement probes the local case for two specific schemes and we do
not generalise from it to either literature.

\textbf{Passive detection.} Reported accuracy transfers poorly once inputs are
perturbed~\cite{hopf2026ntire}; watermark robustness has been benchmarked in
the same spirit~\cite{an2024waves}. Our E0 result is a sharp instance: a
checkpoint advertising $98.70\%$ accuracy scores $\mathrm{AUC}=0.5365$ on our
evaluation distribution. We do not know that checkpoint's training
distribution, so we report the gap rather than explain it.

\textbf{Interference.} The premise this work tests is Wu et
al.'s~\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs} argument that watermarks devised for genuine
images ``may harm the deployed Deepfake detectors ... since the watermarks are
prone to overlap with the forgery signals used for detection'', answered with
AdvMark, fine-tuning the watermark itself. The same pathology is reported
independently in audio~\cite{muller2026watermarkshortcut}. Guo et
al.~\cite{guo2024passiveorwatermark} benchmark passive against watermark-based
detection head-to-head but never combine them, so whether marking degrades the
passive detectors being benchmarked is not asked. Two gaps follow, which this
paper addresses: no prior interference result controls for generic
perturbation sensitivity, and none is tested under a pre-registered null.
Consequences for a fused decision rule remain open; our corpus cannot settle
that (Limitations).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Provenance evidence as a likelihood ratio}
Let the recovered payload contain $k_e$ bit errors out of $L$. Under intact
provenance, errors are channel noise, $k_e \sim \mathrm{Bin}(L, p_0)$; under no
valid provenance the recovered bits are uninformative, $k_e \sim
\mathrm{Bin}(L, 1/2)$. The provenance log-likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation}
  z_P = L\log 2 + k_e \log p_0 + (L - k_e)\log(1-p_0),
  \label{eq:zp}
\end{equation}
with $p_0$ estimated on a held-out clean-transform calibration set. $z_P$ is in
log-odds units and is defined only when a mark is present.

\subsection{Interference and the evidence-transfer estimator}
For watermark scheme $s$ and class $y$,
\begin{align}
  \Delta_\mu(s,y)   &= \mathbb{E}[V \mid W{=}1, S{=}s, Y{=}y] - \mathbb{E}[V \mid W{=}0, Y{=}y],\\
  \Delta_{\mathrm{AUC}}(s) &= \mathrm{AUC}(V; W{=}1, S{=}s) - \mathrm{AUC}(V; W{=}0).
\end{align}
The attenuation analyses of \S\ref{sec:confirm}--\ref{sec:replication} add one
estimator: for each arm $s$ we fit $v_s = a_s + b_s v_{\text{clean}}$ by OLS,
\emph{per arm and per detector separately}, since the question is whether an
arm's slope differs from $1$, not whether arms differ from each other by
assumption. Every fit is \emph{paired} on the same image. Confidence intervals
on $b_s$ and on slope differences are $95\%$ percentile bootstrap over images
(resampling an image moves all its arms together), $800$--$2{,}000$ resamples
depending on the analysis. Where two detectors' slopes are compared
(\S\ref{sec:replication}), both fits use the identical image set, so a
difference in $b_s$ cannot be an artefact of a difference in sample.

\subsection{Fusion and abstention}
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \sigma\!\left(\beta_0 + \beta_1 z_P + \beta_2 V + \beta_3 W
            + \beta_4 (W\!\cdot\!V)\right).
  \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
A specification that treats the two evidence sources as independent sets
$\beta_4 = 0$; $\beta_4$ therefore captures an interaction between provenance
presence and detector evidence \emph{within this specified model}. It is a
model coefficient, not a direct causal measure of interference, and we read it
only as such. With costs
$c_{FN}, c_{FP}, c_R$, Chow's rule~\cite{chow1970reject} gives
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{lo} = \frac{c_R}{c_{FN}}, \qquad
  \tau_{hi} = 1 - \frac{c_R}{c_{FP}},
  \label{eq:chow}
\end{equation}
valid iff $c_R/c_{FN} + c_R/c_{FP} < 1$, and optimal only if $\hat{y}$ is
calibrated --- the dependency this paper is about.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Data.} A public corpus of StyleGAN-generated faces against real
photographs, balanced by class: whole-image synthesis, \emph{not} face-swap or
reenactment, hence ``synthetic-image detection'' throughout (Limitations
states the cost of that substitution). The exploratory pass used $150{+}150$
images; the confirmatory analysis scores $20{,}000$ held-out images across
five arms (clean, two schemes, two matched nulls), and \S\ref{sec:replication}
adds a second detector over $8{,}000$ of them --- $140{,}000$ detector
evaluations total, released as raw per-image scores. Splits are stratified at
the image level (no identity metadata), so confidence intervals are
sample-level bootstraps.

\textbf{Watermarks.} Two schemes spanning the hand-crafted/learned divide,
pretrained: DWT-DCT-SVD (transform-domain) and RivaGAN (learned
encoder/decoder), both at a $32$-bit payload. Measured imperceptibility:
$36.9$\,dB / $0.967$ SSIM and $40.2$\,dB / $0.983$ respectively. For every
image and scheme we additionally generate a payload-free perturbation
binary-searched to match \emph{that image's} PSNR to within $0.5$\,dB; all
interference quantities are net of this arm. We call it the control, the null
arm, or the matched-PSNR null interchangeably. Its limit should be stated
plainly: PSNR matching is not spectral or perceptual matching, so the arm
bounds generic perturbation sensitivity but does not fully isolate every
structural difference between noise and a watermark. The defensible reading of
our result is that the degradation attributed to watermarking is \emph{also}
produced by a payload-free perturbation of equal per-image PSNR, not that no
watermark-specific interference of any kind exists.

\textbf{Detectors.} Six public checkpoints were screened; one, an
EfficientNet-B6, clears the floor of \S\ref{sec:e0}, used zero-shot with raw
logit difference $V=\ell_{\text{fake}}-\ell_{\text{real}}$ rather than softmax
confidence. Its training data is undisclosed, so results are within-model,
within-image deltas. Because one undisclosed checkpoint cannot separate a
property of detection from a property of a model, we add a second detector we
train ourselves: a frozen ImageNet ResNet-18 feature extractor ($512$-d) with
an $\ell_2$-regularised logistic head fitted on the training split alone.
Validation and test are untouched by construction, and the head is
deliberately linear: a weak probe over generic features differs from a
purpose-trained CNN along architecture, training data and capacity at once. We
do not claim it is the most distant possible comparison, nor that the two are
architecturally comparable --- only that if the effect were a property of
detection rather than of one model, a second detector of comparable baseline
discrimination would be a place to find it.

\textbf{Protocol.} The detector floor, the gate threshold on
$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$, and the fallback framing for a null were fixed
before scoring. Every number below is produced by released code from a single
configuration file.

\subsection{Confirmatory result ($n = 20{,}000$)}
\label{sec:confirm}

Chronology, as recorded in a git-timestamped pre-registration, with each step
labelled by its evidential status: (1) \emph{exploratory} --- a pass at
$n{=}300$ on \emph{test} found no effect on ranking; (2) \emph{post-hoc
discovery} --- repairing an unrelated defect exposed $\Delta\mu_{net}$,
previously unexamined, on a 36-image slice, and it was large; (3) because that
observation was post-hoc and cannot be confirmed on the data that produced it,
we wrote a second pre-registration committing H1/H2/H3, their thresholds and
their analysis code before scoring anything further; (4) \emph{confirmatory}
--- the pre-registered hypotheses were then tested on \emph{validation}
($n{=}20{,}000$), which steps (1) and (2) never touched. One \emph{disclosed
deviation} occurred inside step (4): at $2\%$ of the run a
pipeline-verification script incidentally exposed interim values consistent
with H1/H2. The run was not stopped, extended or otherwise altered in
response, no threshold was changed afterwards, and the full deviation log is
released with the code, so the ordering can be checked against git history
rather than taken on trust.

\textbf{On this detector the null arm attenuates and the watermark arms do
not.} The matched-PSNR null shrinks EfficientNet-B6's log-odds evidence toward
zero by $\approx 16\%$ ($b\approx0.84$) with a small intercept
($a=-0.158$/$-0.125$ against a clean-score sd of $7.04$), while both watermark
arms keep a near-unit slope ($b=0.968$/$0.990$). The watermark arms are not
unchanged, however: RivaGAN carries an intercept of $+0.649$, i.e.\ a
translation of the score distribution rather than a contraction of it. The
distinction the slope buys us is exactly this --- the null contracts, RivaGAN
shifts, and $\Delta\mu$ alone would not separate them. All of this is an
empirical statement about one checkpoint; \S\ref{sec:replication} shows the
ordering does not reproduce on the second detector.

Two further points are of a different kind, following from the statistics
rather than from any detector. First, \textbf{a strictly increasing rescaling
leaves AUC exactly unchanged}: for $v' = a + bv$ with $b>0$, ranking is
preserved for any $a$, so AUC is invariant. We verify this structural case in
a unit test. It does not follow that the measured transformation is exactly
affine --- the regression leaves residuals, and the pre-registered
$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$ is small but non-zero ($+0.0049$, $-0.0051$;
Table~\ref{tab:confirm}). The correct reading is that AUC \emph{cannot}
register the pure-attenuation component, not that an observed AUC null proves
the change was pure attenuation. Second, attenuation acts on the quantity
calibration depends on: shrinking log-odds toward zero changes the
score-to-posterior mapping even where ranking is untouched. Whether that
degrades measured calibration, and whether it moves decisions, is tested in
\S\ref{sec:calibration} rather than assumed here.

Effect sizes (clean-score sd $7.04$): net effects of $0.12$--$0.19$ sd, paired
Cohen's $d_z$ of $0.38$/$0.55$ --- moderate, and class-dependent: the null arm
erodes evidence on synthetic images roughly $3\times$ more than on real
ones.

\subsection{Replication on a provenance-disclosed detector}
\label{sec:replication}

detection'' from ``attenuation is a property of this model''. We built a
detector with disclosed provenance --- frozen ImageNet ResNet-18, logistic
head on \emph{training} alone --- and refit the regression on the same
$8{,}000$ images restricted to the identical set both detectors scored
(Table~\ref{tab:replication}, Fig.~\ref{fig:replication}):

Read the slope as follows: $b=1$ is no change relative to the clean score,
$b<1$ is attenuation toward zero, and a \emph{lower} $b$ is \emph{more}
attenuation. \textbf{The ordering reverses between detectors.}
On EfficientNet-B6 the matched-PSNR null sits far below the watermark arm
($b=0.832$ vs.\ $0.966$ for DWT-DCT-SVD; $0.848$ vs.\ $0.987$ for RivaGAN):
the null attenuates substantially more. On the provenance-disclosed detector
the null sits slightly \emph{above} the watermark arm ($0.971$ vs.\ $0.956$;
$0.976$ vs.\ $0.949$), so it attenuates marginally less. The intervals are
tight and disjoint in both directions, so the reversal is not a resolution
artefact.

The obvious objection, that a linear probe is simply too weak to express the
effect, is closed by matched baseline AUC on the same images: $0.8703$
(EfficientNet-B6) against $0.8725$ (ResNet-18 probe), moving near-identically
under every arm. The two are matched in discriminative power, not in
architecture, and we claim only the former. What differs between them is how
the score \emph{distribution} moves, which is what the regression measures and
AUC cannot see.

We therefore withdraw ``imperceptible noise attenuates detector evidence while
watermarking does not'' as a claim about passive detection, and retain it only
as a description of EfficientNet-B6. Why that checkpoint behaves this way is
not settled here; training augmentation and its $528{\times}528$ processing
resolution against the probe's $224{\times}224$ are both plausible and neither
is tested. Two consequences survive. The control arm is more strongly
motivated than before, not less: without it a study reports the null's
$-0.846$ shift as the watermark's, and with it but only one checkpoint it
reports a model-specific artefact as a property of detection. And the two
structural results --- that $\Delta\mu$ conflates attenuation with
translation, and that AUC is exactly blind to monotone attenuation --- are
analytic. They constrain what any such experiment can detect, and no
replication outcome, ours included, bears on them.

\subsection{Payload survival under localised manipulation}
\label{sec:provenance-inert}

The source framework routes ``is the watermark present, and has it been
altered?'' into detection. We supplied the missing manipulation step ---
watermarking an authentic image, \emph{then} splicing a donor region in with
Poisson blending --- and measured BER against manipulated area, for both
schemes, at six coverage levels (Fig.~\ref{fig:ber_area}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/fig_ber_area}
\caption{BER of the recovered payload vs.\ spliced area. The shaded band is
realistic face-replacement coverage; both curves are near-zero across it.}
\label{fig:ber_area}
\end{figure}

\emph{What was measured.} At $10$--$25\%$ coverage, the range a face
replacement occupies, DWT-DCT-SVD recovers its payload exactly (BER $0.0000$)
and RivaGAN recovers it to within $0.0052$--$0.0104$, against a
payload-destruction reference of $0.5$. BER departs from zero only well beyond
that range --- still $0.0026$ (DWT-DCT-SVD) at $40\%$ coverage, and
$0.0443$/$0.0521$ at $60\%$ --- and even obliterating $95\%$ of the image
leaves DWT-DCT-SVD at $0.2630$, short of the destruction reference. The
payload is therefore not uninformative everywhere; it is uninformative over
the coverage range that matters for face replacement, and only weakly
informative well outside it.

\emph{What it suggests.} The pattern is consistent with the spatial redundancy
expected of a robust scheme, rather than with a peculiarity of this corpus:
the redundancy that lets a mark survive JPEG, resizing and re-encoding is the
same redundancy that lets it survive a region being overwritten.

\emph{What is not established.} We tested two schemes and one splice
mechanism at six coverage levels. That is not a class-level result about
robust watermarking, and we do not state it as one; a differently constructed
robust scheme, or a manipulation that disturbs the carrier globally rather
than locally, could behave differently. Within the tested configuration the
consequence for fusion is concrete: the provenance feature is effectively
constant across the manipulation levels of interest, so a fused rule over it
combines one informative signal with one that does not vary. This is also the
design pressure behind the semi-fragile and separable-decoder schemes of
\S\ref{sec:related}.

\subsection{Calibration under attenuation}
\label{sec:calibration}

Attenuation acts on the score-to-posterior mapping; whether it degrades
measured calibration is a separate question, which we test with ECE under
Platt and isotonic calibration~\cite{guo2017calibration}, fit on the
\emph{clean} half of a calibration split and frozen before evaluation
(Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}). Two disclosures
about its status. This analysis was \emph{not} part of pre-registration~\#2,
which committed H1--H3 only; its protocol was fixed before it was run, but it
is a secondary analysis, not a pre-registered one. And it reuses the same
$20{,}000$ validation images the confirmatory test used, so it is not
independent of them. We therefore report it as supporting evidence about the
mechanism, not as a second confirmatory result.

The two calibrators disagree, and we report the disagreement rather than
selecting the one that suits the argument. Under isotonic regression,
attenuation degrades ECE $4$--$5\times$ relative to clean while watermarking
degrades it far less. Under Platt scaling the null arms appear to
\emph{improve}. The disagreement resolves on evidence independent of the
comparison: Platt's ECE on the \emph{clean} arm is already $13\times$
isotonic's (raw logits span $-11.6$ to $+36.8$, sd $=7.04$), so a single
logistic is badly misspecified for these scores before any perturbation is
applied, and shrinking them happens to partially cancel that misspecification.
A $\Delta$ECE measured against a broken baseline measures the baseline.

The claim therefore holds for calibration \emph{quality} and does not hold for
decision \emph{risk}: isotonic DRD is $0.0055$ (clean) against
$0.0145$/$0.0054$ (nulls), small and inconsistent in direction. Attenuation
reaches the probability estimates without reliably changing the decisions
those estimates drive \emph{under the cost model tested here}. We make no
claim that attenuation is decision-neutral under other cost structures, nor
that it always causes decision harm; the cost triple is assumed rather than
elicited from a deployment (Limitations).

\subsection{The fusion comparison is not estimable; an exploratory transform
analysis}
\label{sec:e4e5}

Two distinct points, which we separate deliberately because conflating them
would misrepresent the second.

First, the planned fusion comparison (F0--F5, with and without the
interaction term of Eq.~\ref{eq:fusion}) under localised manipulation is
\emph{not estimable} in this setup. \S\ref{sec:provenance-inert} shows the
provenance feature is effectively constant across the manipulation coverage of
interest, and a coefficient on a feature that does not vary is not a weak
estimate but an undefined one. We therefore report no confirmatory fusion
result, and nothing below is offered as a substitute for it.

Second, and separately, we ran an \emph{exploratory} analysis asking a
narrower question: does the interaction term respond to benign input changes
at all, on otherwise unmanipulated images? This is reported solely to
characterise the term's behaviour, not to establish any fusion claim.

\emph{no bootstrap intervals} --- point estimates only, at a sample size an

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{Scope of the manipulation result.} We supplied the manipulation step
the source framework presupposes and measured the payload surviving it at
realistic coverage (\S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}), so the channel's silence is
measured rather than assumed. The measurement covers two schemes and one
Poisson-blended elliptical splice. It is not a class-level statement about
robust watermarking, and our splice reproduces the provenance mechanics of a
face swap without reproducing a learned swap's detector-facing realism, so it
does not speak to the detectability of modern face swaps.

\textbf{Statistical power.} The $n{=}300$ exploratory pass has AUC standard
error $\approx0.03$, larger than the effects measured, so its null means
``undetectable at this size''. Confirmatory and replication analyses
($n{=}20{,}000$/$8{,}000$) do not have this problem; reported intervals, not
point estimates, bound excluded effect sizes.

\textbf{Synthesis type.} Whole-image GAN synthesis vs.\ real photographs, not
face-swap/reenactment as in standard benchmarks~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics};
detectors keying on generator fingerprints need not generalise to different
manipulation traces.

\textbf{Detector provenance and count.} The strong detector's training data is
undisclosed (mitigated by within-model, within-image deltas); the disclosed
detector is a linear probe on generic features, not an engineered detector.
Two detectors establish non-universality, not which population of detectors
exhibits the effect --- that needs a panel, and \S\ref{sec:e0} shows that
floor-clearing detectors are the scarce resource.

\textbf{Other.} Interference is measured zero-shot; a task-trained detector is
untested. Decision thresholds use an assumed cost triple and swept, not
measured, $\rho$.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{What a null does and does not mean.} We did not detect
\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs}'s effect in our setting, which is a boundary
condition, not a refutation: their detectors are trained for the manipulation
family they evaluate and their images are manipulated \emph{after} marking,
so watermark and forgery signals coexist as their argument describes. Ours are
zero-shot detectors on whole-image synthesis marked after the fact.

\textbf{Our own gate had the asymmetry it warns about.} We gated on
$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$, while the quantity a calibration-centred thesis
needs is $\Delta\mu_{net}$. Every reading of the latter is post-hoc with
respect to that gate, which is why it was re-registered before being tested
rather than reinterpreted in place.

\textbf{One checkpoint is not a detector.} The replication of
\S\ref{sec:replication} is the result we would have preferred to avoid and the
one most worth reporting. Our probe is not the right second detector in any
principled sense --- it is a weak linear head on generic features --- but a
difference measured on a single checkpoint is not yet a finding, and the
cheapest available remedy, a frozen backbone with a linear head trained on a
named split, costs little enough that its absence is hard to justify. The
detector-floor screen serves the same precondition role: a study measuring a
\emph{difference} in detector behaviour assumes the detector behaves at all.

\section{Conclusion}

We set out to measure whether provenance watermarking interferes with passive
synthetic-image detection, and the defensible answer is narrower than the one
we started toward. Against a payload-free control matched per image to the
watermark's PSNR, on $20{,}000$ held-out images, an EfficientNet-B6 detector's
evidence is attenuated by $\approx16\%$ under the control and left largely
intact under watermarking, so the degradation that setup would attribute to
the watermark is not attributable to it on that checkpoint. On a second,
provenance-disclosed detector matched to the first within $0.003$ baseline AUC
on the same images, the effect does not reproduce and the ordering of the two
arms reverses. Across the two models tested, the attenuation is therefore
checkpoint-dependent; which detectors exhibit it is a question two models
cannot answer, and we do not answer it.

What generalises is analytic rather than empirical. A mean-shift statistic
conflates attenuation with translation, and AUC is exactly blind to monotone
attenuation, so a design that measures with $\Delta\mu$ and gates on
$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}$ can misidentify the mechanism and be structurally unable
to register its attenuation component at the same time; a per-arm slope
separates the two where a mean shift cannot. Three
supporting measurements accompany this. Both tested watermarks retain their
payload through a face-replacement-scale splice, providing no detectable
evidence of manipulation at that coverage in our experiment, though BER does
rise at far larger coverage. Attenuation degrades isotonic calibration without
reliably changing decisions under the cost model tested. And a detector-floor
screen found five of six public checkpoints at chance, one advertising
$98.70\%$ accuracy, which is a precondition for trusting any of the above
rather than a finding about public detectors at large.

None of this shows that watermark--detector interference is absent, and none
of it shows that robust watermarks are generally unable to evidence tampering.
What it shows is narrower and more awkward: the single-checkpoint,
no-control, AUC-based apparatus examined here can confuse a watermark effect
with generic perturbation sensitivity, a property of one model with a property
of detection, and a calibration-relevant change in the score distribution with
no change at all.
