\title{Measuring Watermark--Detector Interference: Controls, Score
Attenuation, and Checkpoint Dependence}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Hardik\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}\IEEEauthorrefmark{2} and Yuv Jindal\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}Dept.\ of Computer Science, Indian Institute of Technology Patna, India}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}Dept.\ of Computer Science and Engineering, Vivekananda Institute of Professional Studies -- TC, India}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Prior work reports that embedding a provenance watermark can \emph{harm} a
passive synthetic-image detector inspecting the same pixels. That claim has a
confound: any imperceptible perturbation shifts a detector trained on clean
images, so degradation after marking need not be watermark-specific. We
introduce a control the prior literature does not apply --- a payload-free
perturbation matched per image to the watermark's own PSNR --- and re-measure
the effect on $20{,}000$ held-out images under hypotheses fixed before the
data was scored.

The control reverses the expected conclusion. On an EfficientNet-B6
detector, the degradation attributed to watermarking belongs to the control:
matched-PSNR noise attenuates the detector's log-odds evidence toward zero by
$\approx 16\%$ (slope $0.83$--$0.85$), while watermarking leaves it
essentially intact (slope $0.97$--$0.99$). We then test whether this
attenuation is a property of detection or of one checkpoint, by repeating the
measurement on a second, provenance-disclosed detector (a frozen ImageNet
ResNet-18 with a logistic head fitted only on the training split) matched to
the first on baseline AUC ($0.870$ vs.\ $0.873$) on the same $8{,}000$ images.
\textbf{The asymmetry does not replicate and its sign reverses.} The matched
AUCs close off a weak-detector explanation, so the observed effect is
checkpoint-dependent in our experiments rather than a general property of
passive detection.

What generalises past this dataset is structural, not empirical: with
near-zero intercept, attenuation is a monotone rescaling and is exactly
invisible to AUC, while it still degrades calibration; a mean-shift statistic
cannot distinguish attenuation from translation, but a slope estimate can.
We report two further measurements in support: the tested robust watermark's
payload survives a face-replacement-scale splice at bit error rate $0.0000$,
so the provenance channel we evaluate carries no information about that
manipulation; and a detector-floor precondition found five of six public
checkpoints operating at chance on our distribution, one advertising
$98.70\%$ accuracy at $\mathrm{AUC}=0.54$ in use.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two defences against synthetic media are converging in deployment. Proactive
provenance marking embeds a signal at creation time so origin can be verified
later; passive detection infers manipulation from residual synthesis
artefacts. Systems increasingly run both, and the natural assumption is that
the two supply independent evidence.

They are not independent, because they are computed from the same pixels.
Wu et al.~\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs} report that watermarking models built
for genuine images can \emph{harm} a deployed detector, on the argument that
watermark signals overlap the forgery signals detection relies on; the same
pathology is reported independently in audio~\cite{muller2026watermarkshortcut}.
If that holds generally, it matters well beyond accuracy: a detector whose
score distribution shifts on marked media invalidates any decision threshold
calibrated on unmarked media, which is precisely the traffic a provenance
system exists to protect.

That argument has a confound, and it is load-bearing. A detector trained on
clean images will respond to \emph{any} imperceptible perturbation. Observing
degradation after marking therefore does not establish that anything about the
watermark's structure is responsible --- generic fragility predicts the same
observation. No prior work in this space separates the two.

We do, with a payload-free perturbation matched per image to the watermark's
own PSNR, and we report every interference quantity net of that arm. We also
fix the decision rule in advance: a detector-floor precondition, a gate
threshold, and the framing to adopt if the gate returns a null are all
committed before scoring.

The gate returned a null on ranking --- and an audit of why turned the
result over. Decomposing the statistic we had pre-registered showed the
watermark contributing almost nothing and the \emph{control} carrying the
effect, and that the effect was not a shift at all but an attenuation: at
matched imperceptibility, random perturbation shrank the detector's evidence
toward zero while the watermark did not. Because attenuation with near-zero
intercept is monotone, it is invisible to AUC by construction, which is
precisely why a ranking gate could return a null while a real effect was
present. We report the retraction alongside the corrected mechanism, since the
sequence is itself the argument for the control arm.

That corrected mechanism then failed its own replication, and we report that
too. Every number above comes from a single third-party checkpoint whose
training data is undisclosed, which leaves a reviewer unable to separate
``attenuation is a property of detection'' from ``attenuation is a property of
this model''. We therefore built a detector with disclosed provenance --- a
frozen ImageNet ResNet-18 feature extractor, whose pretraining contains no
deepfake corpus, with a logistic head fitted on the training split alone ---
and repeated the measurement on the same images. The asymmetry does not
appear: the payload-free arm attenuates by $2$--$3\%$, marginally less than
the watermarked arm, with tight and disjoint confidence intervals
(\S\ref{sec:replication}). The two detectors are matched on baseline
$\mathrm{AUC}$ to within $0.003$, so the difference is not weakness; it is a
difference in \emph{how the score distribution moves}, which is the quantity
AUC cannot see and the regression was introduced to measure.

The consequence sets this paper's scope. Sensitivity to matched-PSNR
perturbation is a fact about a checkpoint, not about passive detection, and
the field's usual apparatus --- one public detector, no control arm, AUC as
the outcome --- cannot detect either that the effect belongs to the control or
that it belongs to the model. What we can claim generally is methodological
and structural: which estimand answers this question, which metric is blind to
it, and what a provenance channel built on a robust watermark can and cannot
contribute to it.

Along the way the precondition earned its keep. Five of six public candidate
detectors operate at chance on our evaluation distribution, one of them
advertising $98.70\%$ accuracy on its model card. Without a floor check their
rows would have entered the results indistinguishable from measurements.

This paper's spine is four results in sequence: a control that overturns an
attribution, the structural reason the field's standard metric misses it, a
replication that bounds how far the resulting mechanism generalises, and what
survives that bounding. Three further measurements support this spine without
carrying equal weight in it, and are reported as such rather than as
independent contributions competing for the same claim.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A control that changes the sign of the conclusion.} We report
        every interference quantity net of a payload-free perturbation matched
        per image to the watermark's own PSNR (100\% of images within 0.5\,dB).
        No prior work in this space applies such a control, and without it our
        own headline number would have read as watermark-induced degradation
        when the degradation belongs to the control.
  \item \textbf{The right estimand is a slope, and the standard one is blind
        to it.} The mechanism at issue is attenuation, not translation: a
        perturbation that shrinks a detector's log-odds evidence toward zero.
        We show that a mean-shift statistic cannot distinguish attenuation from
        translation on a distribution with non-zero mean, and that attenuation
        with near-zero intercept is \emph{exactly} invisible to AUC because it
        is monotone --- which is why a ranking-based gate returns a null while
        a real effect is present. Both are analytic, and the second is checked
        by a unit test rather than asserted.
  \item \textbf{A replication failure that bounds the claim}
        (\S\ref{sec:replication}). On an EfficientNet-B6 checkpoint,
        matched-PSNR noise attenuates evidence by $\approx 16\%$ (slope
        $0.83$--$0.85$) while watermarking does not (slope $0.97$--$0.99$). On
        a second detector we trained ourselves, with disclosed provenance and a
        baseline $\mathrm{AUC}$ matched to within $0.003$ on the same $8{,}000$
        images, the asymmetry vanishes and reverses sign. Two detectors
        establish that the effect is checkpoint-dependent in our experiments
        and does not generalise to the second detector tested; they do not by
        themselves identify which population of detectors exhibits it, and we
        do not claim otherwise. Single-detector interference studies ---
        including the one this paper started as --- cannot make even the
        weaker claim.
  \item \textbf{Supporting measurements.} Independently of any detector, the
        tested robust watermark's payload survives a face-replacement-scale
        splice at bit error rate $0.0000$ (\S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}), so
        the provenance channel we evaluate carries no information about that
        manipulation. With a well-specified calibrator, the same attenuation
        degrades ECE $4$--$5\times$ relative to clean while watermarking
        degrades it far less, though it does not reliably move decisions
        under the cost model tested (\S\ref{sec:calibration}); we also report
        that the field's default calibrator, Platt scaling, is misspecified
        enough on raw detector logits to reverse the sign of that comparison.
        And a detector-floor precondition caught five of six public
        checkpoints operating at chance, one advertising $98.70\%$ accuracy at
        $\mathrm{AUC}=0.54$ in use --- reported as a precondition our own
        measurements depended on, not as a benchmarking study in its own
        right. The full protocol, including a disclosed interim-analysis
        deviation and a retraction of our own initial interpretation, is on
        the record in \S\ref{sec:confirm}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Proactive watermarking for deepfake forensics}
A substantial line of work embeds a mark at creation time and reads it back to
establish provenance, increasingly unifying provenance with detection or
localisation in a single scheme~\cite{wu2026allinone,zeng2026lava}. Our concern
is orthogonal to the design of any particular scheme: we ask what embedding a
mark does to a passive detector that inspects the same pixels, and we treat
watermark strength as an experimental axis (one hand-crafted transform-domain
scheme, one learned encoder/decoder) rather than proposing a new one.

A separate and larger line of work responds to exactly the failure mode we
measure in \S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}: robust, spatially redundant
watermarks recover their payload under benign processing precisely because
they recover it under localised tampering too, which makes them structurally
poor at answering ``was this edited''. \emph{Semi-fragile} and
\emph{separable-decoder} schemes are built to break that coupling.
SepMark~\cite{wu2023sepmark} uses two decoders from one encoder --- a robust
Tracer for source attribution and a fragile Detector that disappears under
manipulation --- so the same mark answers both questions rather than
optimising one at the expense of the other. FractalForensics~\cite{wang2025fractalforensics}
and LampMark~\cite{wang2024lampmark} pursue the same semi-fragility from
different embedding constructions (fractal-parameterised watermarks;
training-free landmark-derived watermarks), and EditGuard~\cite{zhang2024editguard}
and its successor OmniGuard~\cite{zhang2025omniguard} reframe the problem as
image-bit steganography to make localisation tamper-agnostic rather than
attack-specific. WaveGuard~\cite{he2025waveguard}, DiffMark~\cite{sun2025diffmark},
and GIFGuard~\cite{che2026gifguard} extend the same localisation-first design
to wavelet/graph, diffusion-conditioned, and GIF-specific embeddings
respectively. None of this literature reports the BER-versus-manipulated-area
curve we measure for a robust scheme (\S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}); it exists
because the failure mode is understood well enough to design around, which is
independent confirmation of the mechanism rather than a measurement of it.

A second, orthogonal failure surface for robust watermarking is
diffusion-based regeneration attacks, which degrade a payload not through
targeted localised editing but through global stochastic transformation.
Zhang et al.'s ZoDiac~\cite{zhang2024zodiac} shows a watermark embedded in a
diffusion model's latent space surviving such attacks better than pixel-space
schemes; Ni et al.~\cite{ni2025diffusionbreaks} and Qi et al.~\cite{qi2026editingaway}
each show, independently and from different theoretical angles (an
information-theoretic recovery bound; a stochastic-contraction argument), that
sufficient diffusion-based editing drives watermark-payload mutual information
toward zero regardless. Together with our BER measurement, this is two
different mechanisms --- global regeneration and local splicing --- converging
on the same conclusion about robust watermarking's limits.

\subsection{Passive detection and its robustness}
Passive detectors infer manipulation from residual synthesis artefacts. Their
robustness under degradation is an active concern, and recent benchmarking
finds that reported accuracy transfers poorly once inputs are
perturbed~\cite{hopf2026ntire}. Watermark robustness has been benchmarked in
the same spirit~\cite{an2024waves}. Our E0 result is a sharp instance of the
transfer problem: a detector advertising $98.70\%$ accuracy operates at chance
on a synthesis distribution it was not tuned for.

\subsection{Interference between the two paradigms}
The premise this work tests is due to Wu et al.~\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs},
who argue that watermarking models devised for genuine images ``may harm the
deployed Deepfake detectors when directly applied to forged images, since the
watermarks are prone to overlap with the forgery signals used for detection'',
and who respond with AdvMark, fine-tuning the \emph{watermark} so it helps
rather than hurts. The same pathology is reported independently in
audio~\cite{muller2026watermarkshortcut}, where detectors latch onto the mark
as a shortcut for ``synthetic''. Cross-modal replication is what makes the
effect worth treating as structural rather than incidental.

Guo et al.~\cite{guo2024passiveorwatermark} benchmark passive against
watermark-based detection head-to-head and conclude the latter dominates, but
theirs is explicitly a comparison: the two paradigms are never combined, and
the question of whether marking degrades the passive detectors being
benchmarked is not asked.

Three gaps follow, and this paper addresses the first two. First, prior
interference results are reported as raw degradation, with no control
separating watermark-specific interference from a detector's generic
fragility under any imperceptible perturbation --- we introduce a
PSNR-matched payload-free arm for exactly this. Second, the effect has not
been tested under a pre-registered null hypothesis; we fix a gate threshold
and a fallback framing in advance and report the outcome that follows. Third,
the consequences for a fused decision rule remain open; our corpus cannot
settle that (see Limitations), and we do not claim to.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Provenance evidence as a likelihood ratio}
Let the recovered payload contain $k_e$ bit errors out of $L$. Under intact
provenance, errors are channel noise, $k_e \sim \mathrm{Bin}(L, p_0)$; under no
valid provenance the recovered bits are uninformative, $k_e \sim
\mathrm{Bin}(L, 1/2)$. The provenance log-likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation}
  z_P = L\log 2 + k_e \log p_0 + (L - k_e)\log(1-p_0),
  \label{eq:zp}
\end{equation}
with $p_0$ estimated on a held-out clean-transform calibration set. $z_P$ is in
log-odds units and is defined only when a mark is present.

\subsection{Interference}
For watermark scheme $s$ and class $y$,
\begin{align}
  \Delta_\mu(s,y)   &= \mathbb{E}[V \mid W{=}1, S{=}s, Y{=}y] - \mathbb{E}[V \mid W{=}0, Y{=}y],\\
  \Delta_{\mathrm{AUC}}(s) &= \mathrm{AUC}(V; W{=}1, S{=}s) - \mathrm{AUC}(V; W{=}0).
\end{align}

The attenuation analyses of \S\ref{sec:confirm}--\ref{sec:replication} add one
further estimator on top of these: for each arm $s$ (a watermark scheme or its
matched null) we fit $v_s = a_s + b_s v_{\text{clean}}$ by ordinary least
squares, \emph{per arm and per detector separately} --- not as a single joint
model with an interaction term, since the question is whether each arm's
slope differs from $1$, not whether arms differ from each other by
assumption. Every fit is \emph{paired}: $v_{\text{clean}}$ and $v_s$ are the
scores of the \emph{same image} under the two arms, never a comparison across
different images or across detectors' own clean baselines. Confidence
intervals on $b_s$ and on differences between two such slopes are $95\%$
percentile bootstrap over images (not over the paired residuals), $800$--$2{,}000$
resamples depending on the analysis, reported as stated at each use; resampling
an image moves all of its arms together, matching pre-registration \#2's
resampling unit for $\Delta_\mu$ and $\Delta_{\mathrm{AUC}}$. Where two
detectors' slopes are compared (\S\ref{sec:replication}), both fits use the
identical image set --- the intersection of images both detectors scored ---
so a difference in $b_s$ cannot be an artefact of a difference in sample.

\subsection{Fusion}
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \sigma\!\left(\beta_0 + \beta_1 z_P + \beta_2 V + \beta_3 W
            + \beta_4 (W\!\cdot\!V)\right).
  \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
The independence assumption implicit in naive fusion forces $\beta_4 = 0$;
$\beta_4 \neq 0$ is therefore the measurement of interference inside the decision
rule.

\subsection{Risk-derived abstention}
With costs $c_{FN}, c_{FP}, c_R$, Chow's rule~\cite{chow1970reject} gives
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{lo} = \frac{c_R}{c_{FN}}, \qquad
  \tau_{hi} = 1 - \frac{c_R}{c_{FP}},
  \label{eq:chow}
\end{equation}
valid iff $c_R/c_{FN} + c_R/c_{FP} < 1$, and optimal only if $\hat{y}$ is
calibrated --- the dependency this paper is about.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.} A public corpus of StyleGAN-generated faces against real
photographs, balanced by class. The exploratory pass used $150{+}150$ images;
the confirmatory analysis scores $20{,}000$ held-out images across five arms
(clean, two watermark schemes, two matched null perturbations), and the
replication of \S\ref{sec:replication} adds a second detector over $8{,}000$
of them --- $140{,}000$ detector evaluations in total, all released as raw
per-image scores. This is
whole-image synthesis, \emph{not} face-swap or reenactment manipulation; we use
``synthetic-image detection'' rather than ``deepfake detection'' throughout,
and Limitations states what that substitution costs. The corpus carries no
identity metadata, so splits are stratified at the image level with a fixed
seed rather than at identity level; confidence intervals are correspondingly
sample-level bootstraps, a weaker guarantee than identity-clustered
resampling.

\paragraph{Watermark schemes.} Two schemes spanning the hand-crafted/learned
divide, both used pretrained with no training on our part: a DWT-DCT-SVD
transform-domain scheme, and RivaGAN, a learned encoder/decoder. Payload
length is fixed at $32$ bits for both so the comparison is at matched
capacity. Measured imperceptibility is $36.9$\,dB PSNR / $0.967$ SSIM for the
former and $40.2$\,dB / $0.983$ for the latter.

\paragraph{Null-perturbation control.} For every image and every scheme we
additionally generate a payload-free perturbation binary-searched to match
\emph{that scheme's PSNR on that image} to within $0.5$\,dB. All interference
quantities are reported net of this arm. Note the residual it does not
control: PSNR matching is not spectral matching, so a mid-frequency
transform-domain mark and our noise model can agree on PSNR while differing
in where their energy sits. The control therefore bounds generic
perturbation sensitivity; it does not fully isolate structure.

\paragraph{Detectors.} Six public checkpoints were screened; one, an
EfficientNet-B6, clears the floor of \S\ref{sec:e0}. It is used zero-shot,
with no fine-tuning, and we take the raw logit difference
$V=\ell_{\text{fake}}-\ell_{\text{real}}$ rather than a softmax confidence,
because softmax confidence is unreliable in exactly the perturbed regime
under study. Its training data is undisclosed, so all reported quantities are
within-model, within-image deltas.

Because one undisclosed checkpoint cannot separate a property of detection
from a property of a model, we add a second detector we train ourselves: a
frozen ImageNet ResNet-18 as feature extractor ($512$-d pooled features) with
an $\ell_2$-regularised logistic head fitted on the corpus training split
alone. Its provenance is disclosed end to end --- ImageNet classification
pretraining contains no deepfake corpus, and the head never sees validation or
test --- so its baseline AUC on those splits is honest by construction rather
than by a contamination diagnostic that can only fail to fire. The head is
deliberately linear: a weak probe over generic features is the most
architecturally distant thing that still clears the floor, which is what makes
a shared effect evidence of generality and a divergent one evidence against
it.

\paragraph{Protocol.} The detector floor (E0), the gate threshold on
$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$, and the fallback framing for a null were fixed
before the data was scored. Every number below is produced by released code
from a single configuration file.

\section{Results}

\subsection{Detector floor check (E0)}
\label{sec:e0}

Before any interference quantity is interpreted, each detector must clear a
baseline AUC floor on unwatermarked data: a detector near chance makes
$\Delta$AUC a measurement of noise, indistinguishable from a genuine null.

We screened every public deepfake image classifier returned by a hub search
(six checkpoints). \textbf{Five of the six operate at chance on our
evaluation distribution.}

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrl}
\toprule
Checkpoint & Baseline AUC & \\
\midrule
EfficientNet-B6 (\texttt{Skullly}) & 0.8952 & clears floor \\
ViT (\texttt{Wvolf}) & 0.5365 & chance; card claims $98.70\%$ \\
\texttt{DaMsTaR/Detecto} & 0.5365 & chance \\
\texttt{prithivMLmods/v2} & 0.5299 & chance \\
\texttt{dima806} & 0.5296 & chance \\
\texttt{Hemg} & 0.4576 & below chance \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Two observations. The \texttt{Wvolf} and \texttt{DaMsTaR} checkpoints agree to
four decimal places across 300 images, which is not plausibly coincidence and
suggests one is a re-upload of the other --- so the pool of genuinely
independent public detectors is smaller than a hub search implies. And a model
scoring below $0.5$ most likely carries an inverted label convention rather
than anti-predictive skill; either way it does not clear the floor.

This matters beyond our setting. Published zero-shot accuracies are quoted on
the distribution a model was tuned for, and the gap between $98.70\%$ on a
card and $\mathrm{AUC}=0.54$ in use is not a rounding error. A precondition
check is the only thing standing between such a checkpoint and a results table
full of confident noise --- and in our case it was the difference between one
usable measurement and five spurious ones.

It also bounds this study: only one public detector clears the floor. No
amount of further screening fixes that --- it is a property of what exists
publicly --- so rather than state it as a limitation we removed it, by
training a second detector whose provenance we can disclose in full
(\S\ref{sec:replication}). That decision turned out to matter more than a
robustness check usually does.

\subsection{Interference, net of a payload-free control (E1)}
\label{sec:e1}

$\Delta$AUC alone cannot distinguish structured, watermark-specific
interference from a detector's generic fragility under \emph{any}
imperceptible perturbation. We therefore report every quantity net of a
payload-free control ($S=\emptyset$) matched per image to the corresponding
watermark's PSNR (\S\ref{sec:method}).

\subsection{Confirmatory result ($n = 20{,}000$)}
\label{sec:confirm}

The chronology, in the order it happened and as recorded in a
git-timestamped pre-registration document: (1) an exploratory pass at
$n{=}300$ on the \emph{test} split found no watermark-specific effect on
detector \emph{ranking} ($\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$ inside a pre-registered
$\pm 0.02$ band); (2) repairing an unrelated defect --- the null arm was
being subtracted from $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}$ but not from $\Delta\mu$ ---
exposed $\Delta\mu_{net}$, a quantity that had not been examined, on a
36-image slice of \emph{test}; it was large; (3) because that observation was
post-hoc with respect to the original gate, we wrote a second pre-registration
committing H1/H2/H3, their thresholds, and their analysis code, before
scoring any further data; (4) the confirmatory hypotheses were tested on the
\emph{validation} split ($n{=}20{,}000$), which step (1) and (2) never
touched. One disclosed deviation occurred inside that step: at $2\%$ of the
validation run, an aggregation script was executed against four completed
shards to verify the pipeline before committing several hours of compute to
it, which incidentally exposed interim $\Delta\mu_{net}$/$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}$
values consistent with H1/H2. The run was not stopped, shortened, or extended
in response, no threshold was altered afterward, and the full deviation log
is released alongside the code. The reader can verify from git history that
every threshold below predates that peek.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
Scheme & $\Delta\mu_{net}$ (H1) & $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$ (H2) \\
\midrule
DWT-DCT-SVD & $\mathbf{+0.837}$ [$+0.807$, $+0.867$] & $+0.0049$ [$+0.0032$, $+0.0066$] \\
RivaGAN     & $\mathbf{+1.353}$ [$+1.317$, $+1.387$] & $-0.0051$ [$-0.0071$, $-0.0033$] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Both pre-registered tests pass: $\Delta\mu_{net}$ excludes zero and exceeds
the $0.10$-logit floor; $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$ stays inside the
$\pm 0.02$ band.

\textbf{But $\Delta\mu_{net}$ is the wrong estimand, and reporting it alone
would misdescribe the mechanism.} An audit decomposing it into its two halves
shows the watermark contributes almost nothing for the hand-crafted scheme:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\toprule
Scheme & watermark $-$ clean & null $-$ clean & net \\
\midrule
DWT-DCT-SVD & $\mathbf{-0.009}$ & $-0.846$ & $+0.837$ \\
RivaGAN & $+0.605$ & $-0.748$ & $+1.353$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The entire net effect for DWT-DCT-SVD is the \emph{control} falling. Nor is
the effect a location shift: it ranges from $-0.36$ to $+2.28$ across
clean-score quintiles, where a translation would be flat. Regressing each
arm on the clean score, $v_{\text{arm}} = a + b\,v_{\text{clean}}$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
\toprule
Arm & slope $b$ & intercept $a$ \\
\midrule
DWT-DCT-SVD & $0.968$ & $+0.125$ \\
null (PSNR-matched) & $\mathbf{0.834}$ & $-0.158$ \\
RivaGAN & $0.990$ & $+0.649$ \\
null (PSNR-matched) & $\mathbf{0.850}$ & $-0.125$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{The mechanism is attenuation.} At matched imperceptibility
($\approx 40$\,dB PSNR, $100\%$ of images within $0.5$\,dB of their scheme),
random perturbation shrinks \emph{this} detector's log-odds evidence toward
zero by $\approx 16\%$ ($b \approx 0.84$, $a \approx 0$), while watermarking
leaves it essentially intact ($b \approx 0.97$--$0.99$).
\S\ref{sec:replication} shows the asymmetry is specific to this checkpoint;
the two consequences below are not, because they are properties of the
statistics rather than of the model. $\Delta\mu$ cannot distinguish
these: attenuating a distribution whose mean is not zero (here $+4.16$) lowers
that mean, so a slope change is reported as a shift.

Two consequences follow, and they are sharper than a location-shift story.
First, \textbf{attenuation is invisible to AUC by construction} --- with
$a \approx 0$ it is a monotone rescaling, and AUC is rank-based; we verify in
a unit test that AUC is \emph{exactly} unchanged under pure attenuation. This
is why our AUC gate returned a null while a real effect was present, and why
any study gating on AUC alone cannot see this class of effect. Second,
\textbf{attenuation is precisely what breaks calibration}: shrinking log-odds
toward zero changes the score-to-posterior mapping, so a threshold calibrated
on unperturbed media is applied to evidence that has been scaled down.

Effect sizes, stated in standardised units rather than raw logits: against a
clean-score standard deviation of $7.04$, the net effects are $0.12$--$0.19$
sd, with paired per-image Cohen's $d_z$ of $0.38$ and $0.55$ --- moderate. The
effect is also class-dependent, which a pooled figure hides: the null arm
erodes evidence on manipulated images roughly three times more than on real
ones ($-1.447$ vs $-0.246$ for DWT-DCT-SVD). Imperceptible noise
preferentially destroys the evidence that something is fake.
\subsection{Replication on a provenance-disclosed detector}
\label{sec:replication}

Everything above rests on one third-party checkpoint whose training data is
undisclosed. That is not a footnote: it makes ``attenuation is a property of
detection'' and ``attenuation is a property of this model'' observationally
identical. We separate them by building a detector whose provenance is known
by construction --- a frozen ImageNet ResNet-18 used as a feature extractor
(ImageNet classification pretraining contains no deepfake corpus) with a
logistic head fitted on the corpus's \emph{training} split alone, so
validation and test are untouched by construction rather than by a diagnostic
that can only fail to detect contamination. The head is deliberately linear.

We scored all five arms on $8{,}000$ validation images and refit
$v_{\text{arm}} = a + b\,v_{\text{clean}}$ for both detectors restricted to
the \emph{identical} images, with $800$-resample bootstrap intervals:

\begin{tabular}{llrr r}
\toprule
Detector & Scheme & watermark $b$ [95\% CI] & null $b$ [95\% CI] & null $-$ wm \\
\midrule
EfficientNet-B6 & DWT-DCT-SVD & $0.966$ [$0.962,0.971$] & $\mathbf{0.832}$ [$0.825,0.839$] & $\mathbf{-0.134}$ \\
EfficientNet-B6 & RivaGAN     & $0.987$ [$0.983,0.992$] & $\mathbf{0.848}$ [$0.841,0.856$] & $\mathbf{-0.139}$ \\
ResNet-18 probe & DWT-DCT-SVD & $0.956$ [$0.951,0.962$] & $0.971$ [$0.967,0.974$] & $\mathbf{+0.014}$ \\
ResNet-18 probe & RivaGAN     & $0.949$ [$0.943,0.954$] & $0.976$ [$0.972,0.979$] & $\mathbf{+0.027}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\textbf{The asymmetry does not replicate, and its sign reverses.} On the
provenance-disclosed detector the payload-free arm attenuates by $2$--$3\%$
--- marginally \emph{less} than the watermarked arm --- against $16\%$ on
EfficientNet-B6. The intervals are tight and nowhere near overlapping in
either direction.

The available escape, that a linear probe is simply too weak to express the
effect, is closed by the baseline numbers on those same images:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
Detector & clean & DWT-DCT-SVD & null[DWT] & RivaGAN & null[Riva] \\
\midrule
EfficientNet-B6 & $0.8703$ & $0.8672$ & $0.8629$ & $0.8586$ & $0.8644$ \\
ResNet-18 probe & $0.8725$ & $0.8641$ & $0.8655$ & $0.8598$ & $0.8665$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The two detectors are equally discriminative here ($\mathrm{AUC}$ $0.8703$
vs.\ $0.8725$) and their AUCs move near-identically under every arm. What
differs is how the score \emph{distribution} moves --- exactly the quantity
AUC cannot see, and exactly what the regression was introduced to measure. A
weakness argument cannot explain a divergence in a quantity on which the two
models are matched.

We therefore withdraw ``imperceptible noise attenuates detector evidence
while watermarking does not'' as a general claim. EfficientNet-B6's
sensitivity to matched-PSNR additive noise is a fact about that model ---
plausibly about its training augmentation, or about a $528\times528$
processing resolution that resamples fine noise differently from the probe's
$224\times224$ --- and we have no basis for asserting it of passive detection
at large. Two things this does not touch. The control arm is now
\emph{more} strongly motivated, not less: a study without it would have
reported the null's $-0.846$ mean shift as the watermark's, and a study with
it but with one detector --- the design of every interference paper we are
aware of, and of this one until this section --- would have reported a
checkpoint artefact as a property of detection. And the two structural
results ($\Delta\mu$ conflates attenuation with translation; AUC is exactly
blind to monotone attenuation) are analytic, so no replication can disturb
them.

\subsection{Robust watermarks cannot see localised manipulation}
\label{sec:provenance-inert}

The framework this work set out to test routes a provenance question ---
``is the watermark present, and has it been altered?'' --- into deepfake
detection. We supplied the manipulation step that question presupposes,
watermarking an authentic image and \emph{then} splicing an elliptical region
of a donor image into it with Poisson blending, and measured the bit error
rate of the recovered payload against the manipulated area.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{rrr}
\toprule
Area manipulated & DWT-DCT-SVD BER & RivaGAN BER \\
\midrule
10\% & $\mathbf{0.0000}$ & $0.0052$ \\
25\% & $\mathbf{0.0000}$ & $0.0104$ \\
40\% & $0.0026$ & $0.0156$ \\
60\% & $0.0443$ & $0.0521$ \\
80\% & $0.1953$ & $0.1224$ \\
95\% & $0.2630$ & $0.1615$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

BER $0.5$ means the payload is destroyed; $0.0$ means it is recovered
perfectly, which is also the unmanipulated baseline. At the coverage a face
replacement actually costs --- roughly $10$--$25\%$ of image area --- the
payload survives \emph{intact}, and the tampered image is indistinguishable
from an untouched one on the provenance channel. Obliterating $95\%$ of the
image still leaves BER at $0.26$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.85\linewidth]{figures/fig_ber_area}
\caption{Bit error rate of the recovered payload as a function of spliced
area, for both schemes. The shaded band marks the area a realistic
face-replacement manipulation covers; both curves are flat and near-zero
across it, well below the destruction threshold.}
\label{fig:ber_area}
\end{figure}

This is not a shortcoming of our corpus. For the one scheme and one
manipulation mechanism we tested --- redundant, whole-image embedding against
a Poisson-blended elliptical splice --- the same redundancy that lets a mark
survive JPEG, resizing and re-encoding is what lets it survive having a
region replaced: robust redundancy that preserves the payload under benign
distortion also preserves it under localised tampering. We do not claim this
of every robust watermarking construction or every manipulation mechanism,
only of the ones measured here, though the mechanism is generic enough that
we would be surprised by a robust, spatially redundant scheme that behaved
otherwise.

Two consequences follow. A fused decision rule over such a channel is fusing
one informative signal with a constant, so the fusion comparison we set out to
run is not merely underpowered here but ill-posed --- a claim we can now
support with a measurement rather than a suspicion. And it explains why
semi-fragile and localisation-capable schemes exist as a separate line of work
rather than as refinements of robust watermarking: they are a response to
exactly this failure (\S\ref{sec:related}). A robust mark answers where content
came from; it cannot answer whether content was edited. Our measurement puts
the crossover at
roughly $40$--$60\%$ manipulated area, far outside any realistic face
manipulation.

\subsection{Calibration under attenuation}
\label{sec:calibration}

Attenuation is invisible to AUC but not to calibration, and that argument
should be measured rather than asserted. We use expected calibration error and
the two calibrators (Platt scaling and isotonic regression) in the sense
standard since Guo et al.~\cite{guo2017calibration}, who established that
modern neural networks are poorly calibrated by default and that post-hoc
calibration is not optional if a score is to be read as a probability.
Protocol, fixed before running: split
the $20{,}000$ scored images in half; fit a calibrator on the \emph{clean arm
of the calibration half only} --- the deployment story, in which one
calibrates once on unmarked media --- freeze it, and apply it to the
evaluation half of every arm.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Arm & Platt ECE & Isotonic ECE & slope vs.\ clean \\
\midrule
clean            & $0.1273$ & $\mathbf{0.0094}$ & $1.000$ \\
DWT-DCT-SVD      & $0.1228$ & $0.0154$ & $0.968$ \\
RivaGAN          & $0.1353$ & $0.0290$ & $0.990$ \\
null[DWT]        & $0.0958$ & $\mathbf{0.0492}$ & $0.834$ \\
null[RivaGAN]    & $0.1009$ & $\mathbf{0.0456}$ & $0.850$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Under isotonic regression, attenuation degrades expected calibration error by
$+0.040$/$+0.036$ --- $4$--$5\times$ the clean baseline --- while the
watermarked arms, at matched imperceptibility, degrade it far less. Under
Platt scaling the null arms \emph{improve}. We report the disagreement rather
than choosing the calibrator that agrees with us, and we resolve it on
grounds independent of the comparison: Platt's ECE \emph{on the clean arm} is
$0.1273$, thirteen times isotonic's. A single logistic in the raw logit is
badly misspecified for this detector (scores span $-11.6$ to $+36.8$ with
$\mathrm{sd}=7.04$), so its baseline is broken before any perturbation is
applied, and attenuation happens to shrink scores in a direction that
partially cancels the misspecification. A $\Delta$ECE against a broken
baseline measures the baseline. That the field's default calibrator is this
misspecified on a real detector's logits is worth reporting on its own.

The claim survives for calibration \emph{quality} and fails for decision
\emph{risk}. Under the cost model of \S\ref{sec:method}, isotonic decision-risk
deviation is $0.0055$ (clean), $0.0145$ (null[DWT]) and $0.0054$
(null[RivaGAN]) --- small and inconsistent in direction. The mechanism reaches
the probability estimates without reliably moving the decisions those
probabilities drive at these costs. We state that rather than a
decision-level harm the data does not show.

\subsection{Why the fusion comparison is not reported}

The framework this work began from fuses a provenance score with a passive
detector score, and the planned experiments compared fusion models with and
without an interference term. We do not report that comparison, and the
reason is \S\ref{sec:provenance-inert} rather than a shortfall of effort or
compute: at face-replacement-scale manipulation the provenance channel's
output is constant. A fusion model given one informative feature and one
constant is not a weak experiment; it is an ill-posed one, and any
$\beta$ coefficient recovered from it would describe our sampling noise. The
honest report is the measurement that made it ill-posed, which is why that
measurement is a result here rather than a limitation.

\subsection{Transform robustness and the $\rho$ sweep: a first look}
\label{sec:e4e5}

This is a different fusion question from \S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}'s: not
whether the provenance channel detects localised manipulation, but whether
the $\beta_4$ interaction term (Eq.~\ref{eq:fusion}) responds to a standard
transform applied after watermarking, on otherwise unmanipulated images. We
report it at reduced scale and flag that reduction rather than let a small
n pass as confirmatory. $n{=}400$ (120 calibration / 280 test, stratified,
held-out \emph{test} split, disjoint from the validation split used
elsewhere), against EfficientNet-B6, across seven transform variants (JPEG
quality $\{90,70,50\}$, resize-and-restore at scale $\{0.75, 0.5\}$,
brightness/contrast $\pm 20\%$) applied to the watermarked image before
detection, and a sweep over $\rho$, the fraction of the evaluation stream
that carries a watermark at all. Unlike every
other quantity in this paper, \emph{no bootstrap interval is computed here}
--- each number is a point estimate on 280 test images. We hold it to that
standard explicitly rather than let the absence of a CI go unstated.

prevalence in the stream is not going to matter either.

\section{Limitations}

These are stated here rather than distributed through the results, because
several of them bound what the results can mean at all.

\paragraph{The provenance channel is inert, and we measured why.}
An earlier draft listed this as a corpus artefact: our images are already real
or synthetic, so the watermark is applied after the fact rather than before a
manipulation, and the manipulation step $D$ in $V=f_\theta(T(D(\tilde{x})))$
was simply absent. We then supplied $D$ --- a Poisson-blended elliptical
region splice, which is what a face swap does geometrically --- in the correct
order, and measured the result (\S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}). The channel is
still inert, for a reason that is a property of robust watermarking rather
than of our data, and that reason is a finding rather than a limitation.
What remains a genuine limitation is that our splice is not a learned face
swap: it is faithful to the provenance mechanics but not to the
detector-facing realism of a state-of-the-art manipulation, so claims here
concern how provenance evidence behaves under localised tampering, not the
detectability of modern face swaps.

\paragraph{Statistical power.} The exploratory pass ran at $n{=}300$, where
the standard error on a single AUC is roughly $0.03$ --- larger than the net
effects measured, so its null means ``no effect detectable at this sample
size''. The confirmatory and replication analyses do not have this problem
($n{=}20{,}000$ and $n{=}8{,}000$, with bootstrap intervals on slopes narrow
enough that the two detectors' estimates are separated by an order of
magnitude more than their widths). Where we report a null, the reported
interval and not the point estimate is what bounds the effect sizes
excluded.

\paragraph{Synthesis type.} The corpus is whole-image GAN synthesis versus real
photographs, not face-swap or reenactment manipulation of the kind in the
standard face-forgery benchmarks (e.g.\ FaceForensics++'s Face2Face, FaceSwap,
DeepFakes and NeuralTextures manipulations~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics}).
Detectors keying on generator fingerprints need not behave the same way on
manipulations that leave different traces, so these results should not be
read as covering ``deepfakes'' in the face-swap sense.

\paragraph{Detector provenance and count.} The strong detector is a
third-party checkpoint whose training data is undisclosed, so contamination
with our evaluation distribution cannot be excluded; we mitigate by reporting
only within-model, within-image deltas, where common-mode memorisation
cancels to first order, and absolute accuracies are context rather than
claims. The second detector is provenance-disclosed by construction, which is
what makes \S\ref{sec:replication} possible, but it is a linear probe on
frozen generic features, not an independently engineered detector. Two models
establish that the attenuation asymmetry is not universal; they do not
establish which population of detectors exhibits it. Characterising that
requires a panel, and building the panel requires detectors that clear a
floor on this distribution --- which \S\ref{sec:e0} shows is the scarce
resource.

\paragraph{Zero-shot setting.} Interference is measured against detectors used
as shipped. Whether the same effect holds for a detector trained for this task
and distribution is untested here.

\paragraph{Cost model and $\rho$.} Decision thresholds derive from an assumed
cost triple rather than one elicited from a deployment, and the real-world
watermarked fraction $\rho$ is unknown and swept rather than measured. We
therefore report realised coverage beside every cost model, since a band that
is optimal on paper can route most traffic to human review.

\paragraph{Cryptographic binding.} The Ed25519 construction corrects a real
weakness in symmetric-key provenance, but its perceptual-hash commitment
exhibits a $7\%$ false-negative rate on real photographs, so it is presented
as a design correction rather than a deployment-ready mechanism.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What a null here does and does not mean.} We set out to measure an
effect reported in the literature and did not detect it in our setting. That
is not a refutation of~\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs}. Their setting differs in
the two respects most likely to matter: their detectors are trained for the
manipulation family they evaluate, and their forged images are manipulated
\emph{after} marking, so watermark and forgery signals coexist in the way the
overlap argument describes. Ours are zero-shot detectors on whole-image
synthesis marked after the fact. The useful reading is therefore a boundary
condition --- interference is not a universal property of marking a detector's
input --- and the boundary is exactly where future work should push.

\paragraph{Ranking versus calibration.} $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}$ answers whether
marking reorders a detector's scores. It is silent on whether marking
\emph{translates} them, and a translation leaves ranking untouched while
invalidating any posterior calibrated on unmarked media. A study gating on
ranking alone can therefore return a null while a calibration-relevant effect
is present. We flag this asymmetry because our own pre-registered gate has it:
the gate tests $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$, and the quantity a
calibration-centred thesis needs is $\Delta\mu_{net}$. We report both, and we
are explicit that any reading of the latter is post-hoc with respect to the
gate --- retargeting a gate after seeing data is how a null becomes a false
positive. The correct response is a fresh pre-registration on
$\Delta\mu_{net}$, not a reinterpretation of this one.

\paragraph{One checkpoint is not a detector.} The replication failure of
\S\ref{sec:replication} is the result we would most like to have avoided and
the one most worth reporting. Interference studies in this space, including
this one until we built a second model, measure a property of ``the
detector'' through a single public checkpoint whose training data nobody
discloses. That design cannot distinguish a mechanism from an artefact, and
the artefact here is not subtle: a $16\%$ attenuation, sign-reversed on a
model matched to within $0.003$ AUC on the same images. The methodological
claim is not that our probe is the right second detector --- it is a weak one,
and Limitations says so --- but that a difference measured on one checkpoint
is not yet a finding, and that the cheapest available fix, a frozen
ImageNet backbone with a linear head, is well within any paper's budget.

\paragraph{Detector floors deserve to be standard.} A precondition check cost
almost nothing and caught a checkpoint operating at chance while advertising
$98.70\%$. Any study measuring a \emph{difference} in detector behaviour
inherits the assumption that the detector behaves at all, and that assumption
is worth one line of code and one row in a table.

\section{Conclusion}

We set out to measure whether provenance watermarking interferes with passive
synthetic-image detection, and the answer we can defend is narrower than the
one we started toward. Against a PSNR-matched payload-free control, on
$20{,}000$ held-out images, an EfficientNet-B6 detector's evidence is
attenuated $\approx 16\%$ by imperceptible noise and left intact by
watermarking --- and against a second detector with disclosed provenance,
matched to within $0.003$ AUC on the same images, that asymmetry vanishes and
reverses. Two detectors are enough to show the effect is checkpoint-dependent
in our experiments and does not generalise to the one we built to test it
against; they are not enough to say which population of detectors it belongs
to, and we do not claim to have shown that. We could establish even the
narrower claim only because we ran the control arm and the second model.
Neither is standard practice in this literature; both changed a conclusion
here.

What generalises is structural. A mean-shift statistic conflates attenuation
with translation, and AUC --- the field's default outcome --- is exactly blind
to monotone attenuation, so a study measuring interference with $\Delta\mu$
and gating on $\Delta\mathrm{AUC}$ can be simultaneously wrong about the
mechanism and unable to see it. A slope estimate is the estimand this question
needs. Independently of any detector, the robust watermark we tested carries
no information about the manipulation it is meant to help flag: a
face-replacement-scale edit leaves the payload recovered at bit error rate
$0.0000$, so a fused decision rule over this provenance channel would combine
one informative source with a constant. A detector-floor check that caught
five of six public detectors at chance, one advertising $98.70\%$ accuracy,
was a precondition for trusting any of the above --- not a separate finding
about the state of public benchmarking, though it does say something about
that too.

The useful reading is not that interference is absent. It is that the
apparatus commonly used to look for it --- one public checkpoint, no
payload-free control, AUC as the outcome --- cannot distinguish an effect
from its control, a model from its architecture family, or a real
calibration-relevant change from a null.
