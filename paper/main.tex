\title{Spectral Confounding in Watermark--Detector Interference:
PSNR-Matched Controls Are Not Sufficient}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Hardik\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}\IEEEauthorrefmark{2} and Yuv Jindal\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{1}Dept.\ of Computer Science, Indian Institute of Technology Patna, India}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\IEEEauthorrefmark{2}Dept.\ of Computer Science and Engineering, Vivekananda Institute of Professional Studies -- TC, India}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Prior work reports that embedding a provenance watermark can \emph{harm} a
passive synthetic-image detector inspecting the same pixels. Testing that claim
requires a control, because a detector trained on clean images responds to any
imperceptible perturbation; the natural control, and the one we used
initially, is a payload-free perturbation matched per image to the watermark's
PSNR. On $20{,}000$ held-out images that control appears to settle the
question: matched-PSNR noise attenuates an EfficientNet-B6 detector's log-odds
evidence by $\approx 16\%$ (slope $0.83$--$0.85$) while watermarking leaves it
near the clean-score identity ($0.97$--$0.99$), so the degradation is not the
watermark's.

\textbf{That conclusion does not survive an adequate control.} PSNR fixes only the
\emph{energy} of a perturbation, not where that energy sits in frequency. We
measure the residual spectra and find the PSNR-matched control is spectrally
\emph{inverted} relative to the watermark it stands in for: both tested
watermarks place $0.83$--$0.97$ of their residual power in the lowest radial
band, while their PSNR-matched controls place $0.46$ in the highest. We
therefore build a stronger control --- payload-free, but carrying the
watermark's own per-image power spectrum via phase randomisation --- and
verify it is both payload-free (recovered BER $0.51$--$0.52$, chance) and
matched (PSNR within $0.3$\,dB, SSIM closer to the watermark than the old
control). Against it, on $800$ held-out images, \textbf{the gap closes on
EfficientNet-B6}: the watermark--control slope gap falls from $+0.126$
[$+0.105,+0.146$] to $+0.006$ [$-0.009,+0.021$]. On this checkpoint we detect
no watermark-specific interference: the remaining gap is not distinguishable
from zero at this sample size.

\textbf{That closure does not generalise.} Scoring the identical $800$-image
slice through two more disclosed-provenance probes --- a modern convolutional
network (ConvNeXt-Base) and a vision transformer (ViT-B/16), frozen-backbone
linear heads trained the same way as the ResNet-18 probe below --- shows the
spectrum-matched gap is architecture-dependent, not a property of adequate
control construction in general. The modern CNN keeps a small, reliable,
same-signed gap on both watermarking schemes even against the spectrum-matched
control ($+0.039$ [$+0.025,+0.054$], $+0.033$ [$+0.017,+0.052$], both excluding
zero); the transformer reverses which arm attenuates more \emph{before}
spectrum matching is applied at all (wm$-$null $-0.153$, $-0.239$, watermark
attenuating more than the PSNR-matched null, the opposite ordering from
EfficientNet-B6). We report the fracture and do not have an experiment that
isolates its cause.

A band-limited dose-response, run on EfficientNet-B6 only, establishes
frequency-dependent detector sensitivity on that checkpoint: at fixed PSNR
and zero payload, attenuation grows monotonically with the perturbation's
frequency band ($0.908$, $0.868$, $0.809$ for low, mid, high). Frequency band
alone does not fully account for the watermark, which attenuates less than
even low-band noise; what tracks it is the \emph{fidelity} of the spectral
match: across two samples the control's slope rises from $0.838$ (PSNR-only)
through $0.908$ (band-limited) to $0.958$ (full spectrum-matched) against the
watermark's $0.964$, an indicative ordering rather than a single fitted trend.
The original PSNR-only asymmetry itself is real but belongs to one checkpoint
in the same narrow sense: it does not appear on the ResNet-18 probe in a
full-scale, baseline-AUC-matched replication, and a resolution ablation
holding architecture, data and training fixed excludes input resolution over
the tested range on that pair. It does, differently, on the wider panel above.
Two
analytic results survive independently: a mean-shift statistic cannot
distinguish attenuation from translation, and a strictly increasing rescaling
leaves AUC exactly unchanged, so a ranking-based gate cannot register
attenuation at all.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Two defences against synthetic media are converging in deployment. Proactive
provenance marking embeds a signal at creation time so origin can be verified
later; passive detection infers manipulation from residual synthesis
artefacts. Both read the same pixels, so they are not independent. Wu et
al.~\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs} report that watermarking models built for
genuine images can \emph{harm} a deployed detector, on the argument that
watermark signals overlap the forgery signals detection relies on. The same
pathology is reported independently in audio~\cite{muller2026watermarkshortcut}.
If that holds generally, a detector's score distribution shifting on marked
media invalidates any decision threshold calibrated on unmarked media.

That argument has a load-bearing confound: a detector trained on clean images
responds to \emph{any} imperceptible perturbation, so degradation after
marking need not implicate the watermark's structure. No prior work in this
space separates the two. We do, with a payload-free perturbation matched per
image to the watermark's own PSNR, reporting every interference quantity net
of that arm, with a detector-floor precondition and a gate threshold fixed
before scoring.

Our pre-registered gate returned a null on ranking, and auditing why turned
the result over. The watermark contributed almost nothing; the \emph{control}
carried the effect, and carried it not as a shift but as an attenuation of the
detector's evidence toward zero. A strictly increasing rescaling of scores
leaves AUC exactly unchanged, so a ranking gate is structurally unable to
register the attenuation component of such a change --- which is why a null on
ranking is consistent with a real change in the score distribution.

The natural reading at that point was that watermarking is benign where
imperceptible noise is not. That reading is wrong, and the control is why.
PSNR constrains how much energy a perturbation carries, not where in the
frequency plane it sits. Two perturbations can therefore satisfy the matching
criterion while being structurally unrelated, and measuring the residual
spectra shows that is what happened. Both tested watermarks put $0.83$--$0.97$
of their residual power in the lowest radial band. The PSNR-matched controls
standing in for them put $0.46$ in the highest (\S\ref{sec:spectral}). The
control was not a weaker watermark; it was closer to the opposite perturbation
with the same energy budget.

So we built the control the comparison actually needs. It is payload-free but
carries the watermark's \emph{own} per-image power spectrum, obtained by
randomising the phase of the watermark's residual. Both properties are checked
rather than assumed. The payload is unrecoverable from it (BER $0.51$--$0.52$,
chance), and it matches the watermark on PSNR to within $0.3$\,dB and on SSIM
more closely than the control it replaces. \textbf{Against it the gap closes}
(\S\ref{sec:control-hierarchy}): the watermark--control slope gap on
EfficientNet-B6 falls from $+0.126$ [$+0.105,+0.146$], which excludes zero, to
$+0.006$ [$-0.009,+0.021$], which does not. Once the control is adequate, no
watermark-specific interference is detectable at this sample size.

What remains is a real but narrower phenomenon: on EfficientNet-B6,
attenuation increases monotonically with the perturbation's frequency band at
fixed PSNR (\S\ref{sec:frequency}), and neither watermark produces much
high-frequency energy. That behaviour does
not appear on a second, provenance-disclosed detector matched to it on
baseline AUC (\S\ref{sec:replication}), and a resolution ablation holding
architecture, training data and head fixed shows that input resolution, over
the tested range, does not explain the asymmetry (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}). We report the mechanism as narrowed, not
solved.

This paper's spine is four results. Three supporting measurements follow them
and are reported as support, not as further claims.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The control determines the conclusion, and PSNR is not enough}
        (\S\ref{sec:spectral}). We measure what a PSNR-matched control
        actually matches and find it spectrally inverted relative to the
        watermark: $0.83$--$0.97$ of watermark residual power in the lowest
        band against $0.46$ in the highest for its control. This is a
        measurement of the standard control, not an argument about it.
  \item \textbf{With an adequate control, the interference effect is no longer
        detectable}
        (\S\ref{sec:control-hierarchy}). Against a payload-free,
        spectrum-matched control the watermark--control gap falls to
        $+0.006$ [$-0.009,+0.021$] on EfficientNet-B6 and is not distinguishable
        from zero for either scheme. We verify the new arm is payload-free and
        matched rather than assuming it.
  \item \textbf{The right estimand, and a metric that cannot see it.} A
        mean-shift statistic cannot distinguish attenuation from translation;
        a per-arm slope can. Separately, a strictly increasing rescaling leaves
        AUC exactly unchanged (unit tested), so a ranking-based gate cannot
        register attenuation. Both are analytic and independent of any
        detector.
  \item \textbf{The residual phenomenon is checkpoint-specific, and not
        explained by resolution} (\S\ref{sec:replication},
        \S\ref{sec:mechanism}). EfficientNet-B6's frequency-dependent
        attenuation, measured as a dose-response over three bands at fixed
        PSNR (\S\ref{sec:frequency}),
        does not appear on a second detector matched to within $0.003$ baseline
        AUC, and a resolution sweep from $224$ to $448$ leaves the
        watermark--control contrast flat at $+0.005$ to $+0.009$, against
        $-0.126$ at $528$. Resolution is tested and excluded; architecture,
        training data and augmentation remain untested.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{Proactive watermarking.} A substantial line of work embeds a mark at
creation time to establish provenance, increasingly unifying provenance with
detection or localisation~\cite{wu2026allinone,zeng2026lava}. Our concern is
orthogonal: what embedding a mark does to a passive detector inspecting the
same pixels, treating watermark strength (one hand-crafted, one learned
scheme) as an experimental axis rather than proposing a new one.

A larger line of work is organised around the tension we measure one instance
of in \S\ref{sec:provenance-inert}: robustness to benign processing and
sensitivity to malicious editing pull in opposite directions, so a mark built
to survive re-encoding is not thereby a good answer to ``was this edited''.
Semi-fragile and separable-decoder designs target localisation explicitly
rather than treating it as a by-product of
robustness~\cite{wu2023sepmark,wang2025fractalforensics,wang2024lampmark,zhang2024editguard,zhang2025omniguard,he2025waveguard,sun2025diffmark,che2026gifguard};
a separate failure surface, diffusion-based regeneration, degrades a payload
globally rather than
locally~\cite{zhang2024zodiac,ni2025diffusionbreaks,qi2026editingaway}. Our
splice measurement probes the local case for two specific schemes and we do
not generalise from it to either literature.

\textbf{Passive detection.} Reported accuracy transfers poorly once inputs are
perturbed~\cite{hopf2026ntire}; watermark robustness has been benchmarked in
the same spirit~\cite{an2024waves}. Our E0 result is a sharp instance: a
checkpoint advertising $98.70\%$ accuracy scores $\mathrm{AUC}=0.5365$ on our
evaluation distribution. We do not know that checkpoint's training
distribution, so we report the gap rather than explain it.

\textbf{Interference.} The premise this work tests is Wu et
al.'s~\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs} argument that watermarks devised for genuine
images ``may harm the deployed Deepfake detectors ... since the watermarks are
prone to overlap with the forgery signals used for detection'', answered with
AdvMark, fine-tuning the watermark itself. The same pathology is reported
independently in audio~\cite{muller2026watermarkshortcut}. Guo et
al.~\cite{guo2024passiveorwatermark} benchmark passive against watermark-based
detection head-to-head but never combine them, so whether marking degrades the
passive detectors being benchmarked is not asked. Two gaps follow, which this
paper addresses: no prior interference result controls for generic
perturbation sensitivity, and none is tested under a pre-registered null.
Consequences for a fused decision rule remain open; our corpus cannot settle
that (Limitations).

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Provenance evidence as a likelihood ratio}
Let the recovered payload contain $k_e$ bit errors out of $L$. Under intact
provenance, errors are channel noise, $k_e \sim \mathrm{Bin}(L, p_0)$; under no
valid provenance the recovered bits are uninformative, $k_e \sim
\mathrm{Bin}(L, 1/2)$. The provenance log-likelihood ratio is
\begin{equation}
  z_P = L\log 2 + k_e \log p_0 + (L - k_e)\log(1-p_0),
  \label{eq:zp}
\end{equation}
with $p_0$ estimated on a held-out clean-transform calibration set. $z_P$ is in
log-odds units and is defined only when a mark is present.

\subsection{Interference and the evidence-transfer estimator}
For watermark scheme $s$ and class $y$,
\begin{align}
  \Delta_\mu(s,y)   &= \mathbb{E}[V \mid W{=}1, S{=}s, Y{=}y] - \mathbb{E}[V \mid W{=}0, Y{=}y],\\
  \Delta_{\mathrm{AUC}}(s) &= \mathrm{AUC}(V; W{=}1, S{=}s) - \mathrm{AUC}(V; W{=}0).
\end{align}
The attenuation analyses of \S\ref{sec:confirm}--\ref{sec:replication} add one
estimator: for each arm $s$ we fit $v_s = a_s + b_s v_{\text{clean}}$ by OLS,
\emph{per arm and per detector separately}, since the question is whether an
arm's slope differs from $1$, not whether arms differ from each other by
assumption. Every fit is \emph{paired} on the same image. Confidence intervals
on $b_s$ and on slope differences are $95\%$ percentile bootstrap over images
(resampling an image moves all its arms together), $800$--$2{,}000$ resamples
depending on the analysis. Where two detectors' slopes are compared
(\S\ref{sec:replication}), both fits use the identical image set, so a
difference in $b_s$ cannot be an artefact of a difference in sample.

\subsection{Fusion and abstention}
\begin{equation}
  \hat{y} = \sigma\!\left(\beta_0 + \beta_1 z_P + \beta_2 V + \beta_3 W
            + \beta_4 (W\!\cdot\!V)\right).
  \label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
A specification that treats the two evidence sources as independent sets
$\beta_4 = 0$; $\beta_4$ therefore captures an interaction between provenance
presence and detector evidence \emph{within this specified model}. It is a
model coefficient, not a direct causal measure of interference, and we read it
only as such. With costs
$c_{FN}, c_{FP}, c_R$, Chow's rule~\cite{chow1970reject} gives
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{lo} = \frac{c_R}{c_{FN}}, \qquad
  \tau_{hi} = 1 - \frac{c_R}{c_{FP}},
  \label{eq:chow}
\end{equation}
valid iff $c_R/c_{FN} + c_R/c_{FP} < 1$, and optimal only if $\hat{y}$ is
calibrated --- the dependency this paper is about.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Data.} A public corpus of StyleGAN-generated faces against real
photographs, balanced by class: whole-image synthesis, \emph{not} face-swap or
reenactment, hence ``synthetic-image detection'' throughout (Limitations
states the cost of that substitution). The exploratory pass used $150{+}150$
images; the confirmatory analysis scores $20{,}000$ held-out images across
five arms (clean, two schemes, two matched nulls), and \S\ref{sec:replication}
adds a second detector over $8{,}000$ of them --- $140{,}000$ detector
evaluations total, released as raw per-image scores. Splits are stratified at
the image level (no identity metadata), so confidence intervals are
sample-level bootstraps.

\textbf{Watermarks.} Two schemes spanning the hand-crafted/learned divide,
pretrained: DWT-DCT-SVD (transform-domain) and RivaGAN (learned
encoder/decoder), both at a $32$-bit payload. Measured imperceptibility:
$36.9$\,dB / $0.967$ SSIM and $40.2$\,dB / $0.983$ respectively. For every
image and scheme we generate \emph{two} payload-free controls.

The first is the standard one: additive noise binary-searched to match
\emph{that image's} watermark PSNR to within $0.5$\,dB. We call it the
PSNR-matched control or the null arm.

The second is introduced here because the first is insufficient. PSNR
constrains a perturbation's energy and nothing about its distribution over
frequency, so an arm can satisfy it while being structurally unlike the
watermark. We therefore build a \emph{spectrum-matched} control: take the
watermark's own residual $r = \tilde{x} - x$ on this image, compute its 2-D
Fourier transform, keep the magnitude exactly and replace the phase with
uniform random phase, then invert. The result carries the watermark's power
spectrum per image and per channel, carries no payload, and retains no spatial
structure from the mark; a scalar gain is then binary-searched to land PSNR in
the same tolerance, so the two controls differ in spectrum matching and in
nothing else. We verify rather than assume both properties: payload
unrecoverable at chance BER, and realised PSNR/SSIM reported in
\S\ref{sec:control-hierarchy}.

Neither control makes the comparison perceptually or semantically exact, and
we do not claim it does. What the pair licenses is a sharper statement than
either alone: whether an apparent watermark effect survives as the control is
matched on progressively more of the perturbation's structure.

\textbf{Detectors.} Six public checkpoints were screened; one, an
EfficientNet-B6, clears the floor of \S\ref{sec:e0}, used zero-shot with raw
logit difference $V=\ell_{\text{fake}}-\ell_{\text{real}}$ rather than softmax
confidence. Its training data is undisclosed, so results are within-model,
within-image deltas. Because one undisclosed checkpoint cannot separate a
property of detection from a property of a model, we add three detectors we
train ourselves: frozen, ImageNet-pretrained feature extractors with an
$\ell_2$-regularised logistic head fitted on the training split alone,
spanning three architecture families --- ResNet-18 ($512$-d, global-pooled),
ConvNeXt-Base ($1024$-d, global-pooled) and ViT-B/16 ($768$-d, CLS token).
Validation and test are untouched by construction for all three, and every
head is deliberately linear: a weak probe over generic features differs from
a purpose-trained CNN along architecture, training data and capacity at once.
This is a confound we do not remove, only disclose: EfficientNet-B6 is
fine-tuned on an undisclosed deepfake corpus, while the other three are frozen
probes fitted only on this corpus's train split, so a difference among the
four is not cleanly attributable to architecture alone, and a difference among
the three probes (which share the training regime and differ only in
backbone) is the cleaner comparison where one is needed. We do not claim any
of the probes are the most distant possible comparison, nor that all four are
architecturally comparable --- only that if the effect were a property of
detection rather than of one model, a panel of detectors of comparable
baseline discrimination would be a place to find it, and where it appears
differently across that panel (\S\ref{sec:control-hierarchy}), that too is
reported rather than resolved to a single verdict.

\textbf{Protocol.} The detector floor, the gate threshold on
$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$, and the fallback framing for a null were fixed
before scoring. Every number below is produced by released code from a single
configuration file.

\subsection{Confirmatory result ($n = 20{,}000$)}
\label{sec:confirm}

Chronology, as recorded in a git-timestamped pre-registration, with each step
labelled by its evidential status: (1) \emph{exploratory} --- a pass at
$n{=}300$ on \emph{test} found no effect on ranking; (2) \emph{post-hoc
discovery} --- repairing an unrelated defect exposed $\Delta\mu_{net}$,
previously unexamined, on a 36-image slice, and it was large; (3) because that
observation was post-hoc and cannot be confirmed on the data that produced it,
we wrote a second pre-registration committing H1/H2/H3, their thresholds and
their analysis code before scoring anything further; (4) \emph{confirmatory}
--- the pre-registered hypotheses were then tested on \emph{validation}
($n{=}20{,}000$), which steps (1) and (2) never touched. One \emph{disclosed
deviation} occurred inside step (4): at $2\%$ of the run a
pipeline-verification script incidentally exposed interim values consistent
with H1/H2. The run was not stopped, extended or otherwise altered in
response, no threshold was changed afterwards, and the full deviation log is
released with the code, so the ordering can be checked against git history
rather than taken on trust.

\textbf{On this detector the null arm attenuates and the watermark arms do
not.} The matched-PSNR null shrinks EfficientNet-B6's log-odds evidence toward
zero by $\approx 16\%$ ($b\approx0.84$) with a small intercept
($a=-0.158$/$-0.125$ against a clean-score sd of $7.04$), while both watermark
arms keep a near-unit slope ($b=0.968$/$0.990$). The watermark arms are not
unchanged, however: RivaGAN carries an intercept of $+0.649$, i.e.\ a
translation of the score distribution rather than a contraction of it. The
distinction the slope buys us is exactly this --- the null contracts, RivaGAN
shifts, and $\Delta\mu$ alone would not separate them. All of this is an
empirical statement about one checkpoint; \S\ref{sec:replication} shows the
ordering does not reproduce on the second detector.

Two further points are of a different kind, following from the statistics
rather than from any detector. First, \textbf{a strictly increasing rescaling
leaves AUC exactly unchanged}: for $v' = a + bv$ with $b>0$, ranking is
preserved for any $a$, so AUC is invariant. We verify this structural case in
a unit test. It does not follow that the measured transformation is exactly
affine --- the regression leaves residuals, and the pre-registered
$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$ is small but non-zero ($+0.0049$, $-0.0051$;
Table~\ref{tab:confirm}). The correct reading is that AUC \emph{cannot}
register the pure-attenuation component, not that an observed AUC null proves
the change was pure attenuation. Second, attenuation acts on the quantity
calibration depends on: shrinking log-odds toward zero changes the
score-to-posterior mapping even where ranking is untouched. Whether that
degrades measured calibration, and whether it moves decisions, is tested in
\S\ref{sec:calibration} rather than assumed here.

Effect sizes (clean-score sd $7.04$): net effects of $0.12$--$0.19$ sd, paired
Cohen's $d_z$ of $0.38$/$0.55$ --- moderate, and class-dependent: the null arm
erodes evidence on synthetic images roughly $3\times$ more than on real
ones.

\subsection{Replication on a provenance-disclosed detector}
\label{sec:replication}

detection'' from ``attenuation is a property of this model''. We built a
detector with disclosed provenance --- frozen ImageNet ResNet-18, logistic
head on \emph{training} alone --- and refit the regression on the same
$8{,}000$ images restricted to the identical set both detectors scored
(Table~\ref{tab:replication}, Fig.~\ref{fig:replication}):

The obvious objection, that a linear probe is simply too weak to express the
effect, is closed by matched baseline AUC on the same images: $0.8703$
(EfficientNet-B6) against $0.8725$ (ResNet-18 probe), moving near-identically
under every arm. The two are matched in discriminative power, not in
architecture, and we claim only the former. What differs between them is how
the score \emph{distribution} moves, which is what the regression measures and
AUC cannot see.

We therefore withdraw ``imperceptible noise attenuates detector evidence while
watermarking does not'' as a claim about passive detection, and retain it only
as a description of EfficientNet-B6. Why that checkpoint behaves this way is
not settled here; training augmentation and its $528{\times}528$ processing
resolution against the probe's $224{\times}224$ are both plausible and neither
is tested. Two consequences survive. The control arm is more strongly
motivated than before, not less: without it a study reports the null's
$-0.846$ shift as the watermark's, and with it but only one checkpoint it
reports a model-specific artefact as a property of detection. And the two
structural results --- that $\Delta\mu$ conflates attenuation with
translation, and that AUC is exactly blind to monotone attenuation --- are
analytic. They constrain what any such experiment can detect, and no
replication outcome, ours included, bears on them.

two schemes agree with each other where EfficientNet-B6's controls did not
need to. ViT-B/16 differs in kind, not just degree: its wm$-$null sign is
\emph{reversed} from EfficientNet-B6 ($-0.153$, $-0.239$ -- the watermark
attenuates evidence \emph{more} than the PSNR-matched null, not less), and
while spectrum matching shrinks both gaps by roughly an order of magnitude, it
still excludes zero for DWT-DCT-SVD. No single story --- ``the control was
inadequate'' or ``the effect is real'' --- covers all four rows.

The controls are matched in fact, for every detector, because the arms
themselves do not change across detectors -- only which model scores them.
Realised PSNR is $39.84$ (watermark), $39.84$ (PSNR-matched) and $39.83$
(spectrum-matched) for DWT-DCT-SVD, and $40.53$/$40.52$/$40.45$ for RivaGAN.
SSIM is \emph{closer} for the new control than the old one ($0.978$ vs.\
$0.962$, against a watermark at $0.986$), so the stronger control is not
winning by being a gentler perturbation, and this holds identically whichever
detector's column is read.

We therefore withdraw the reading that imperceptible noise attenuates detector
evidence while watermarking does not, as a general claim about passive
detection. On EfficientNet-B6 specifically, the defensible statement is
narrower and concerns frequency: attenuation grows with the perturbation's
frequency band at fixed PSNR (\S\ref{sec:frequency}), neither tested watermark
produces much high-frequency energy, and a payload-free perturbation matched
to the watermark's full spectrum is indistinguishable from the watermark on
that checkpoint. On the other three, a real but smaller and differently-signed
gap persists against even the strongest control we built, and we do not have
an experiment that explains why (\S\ref{sec:discussion-architecture}).

\subsection{A frequency dose-response, and what it does not explain}
\label{sec:frequency}

control's spectrum, but two spectral conditions do not establish a frequency
response: the same data are consistent with sensitivity to some property
merely correlated with our high-pass noise. We therefore measured the
dose-response directly, with payload-free perturbations confined to a single
radial band, each binary-searched to the watermark's per-image PSNR
($39.77$ against the watermark's $39.80$), on $400$ further held-out images.

\subsection{Mechanism: resolution is tested and excluded}
\label{sec:mechanism}

Why does one checkpoint show this frequency-dependent attenuation and the
other not? The candidate we could isolate is input resolution. Corpus images
are $256\times256$; EfficientNet-B6 processes at $528$, upsampling and
preserving fine perturbation, while the probe processes at $224$, downsampling
and averaging it away. That predicts the watermark--control contrast should
swing negative as resolution rises.

We tested it with everything else held fixed --- same backbone weights, same
training data, same head, same split --- varying only the resolution at which
features are extracted, for both the probe fit and the scoring.

\subsection{Payload survival under localised manipulation}
\label{sec:provenance-inert}

The source framework routes ``is the watermark present, and has it been
altered?'' into detection. We supplied the manipulation step that question
presupposes and asked where the payload actually fails. One mechanism cannot
separate ``robust watermarks survive localised editing'' from ``this splice
happens not to disturb the carrier'', so we test four families on deliberately
different axes: \emph{splice} (foreign content Poisson-blended in),
\emph{inpaint} (region reconstructed from surrounding context),
\emph{copy-move} (the image's own content relocated over the region), and
\emph{local regeneration} (fine structure destroyed in place by blur and
requantisation). Each is swept over a $5$--$100\%$ coverage grid for both
schemes, on $200$ held-out images: $9{,}600$ measurements. No detector is
involved; BER is measured by extraction alone.

\emph{What was measured.} Across all eight family--scheme combinations the

consistent with the spatial redundancy expected of a robust scheme. The
mechanism matters only once coverage is far past anything a face replacement
requires.

are not the class of robust watermarking, nor the class of manipulations. Our
local regeneration is a blur-and-requantise stand-in, not a learned generative
edit, and none of these reproduce a modern face swap's detector-facing
realism. The consequence we do draw is local to the tested configuration: the
provenance feature is effectively constant over the coverage range of
interest, so a fused rule over it combines one informative signal with one
that does not vary.

\subsection{Calibration under attenuation}
\label{sec:calibration}

Attenuation acts on the score-to-posterior mapping; whether it degrades
measured calibration is a separate question, which we test with ECE under
Platt and isotonic calibration~\cite{guo2017calibration}, fit on the
\emph{clean} half of a calibration split and frozen before evaluation
(Table~\ref{tab:calibration}, Fig.~\ref{fig:calibration}). Two disclosures
about its status. This analysis was \emph{not} part of pre-registration~\#2,
which committed H1--H3 only; its protocol was fixed before it was run, but it
is a secondary analysis, not a pre-registered one. And it reuses the same
$20{,}000$ validation images the confirmatory test used, so it is not
independent of them. We therefore report it as supporting evidence about the
mechanism, not as a second confirmatory result.

\subsection{The fusion comparison is not estimable; an exploratory transform
analysis}
\label{sec:e4e5}

Two distinct points, which we separate deliberately because conflating them
would misrepresent the second.

First, the planned fusion comparison (F0--F5, with and without the
interaction term of Eq.~\ref{eq:fusion}) under localised manipulation is
\emph{not estimable} in this setup. \S\ref{sec:provenance-inert} shows the
provenance feature is effectively constant across the manipulation coverage of
interest, and a coefficient on a feature that does not vary is not a weak
estimate but an undefined one. We therefore report no confirmatory fusion
result, and nothing below is offered as a substitute for it.

Second, and separately, we ran an \emph{exploratory} analysis asking a
narrower question: does the interaction term respond to benign input changes
at all, on otherwise unmanipulated images? This is reported solely to
characterise the term's behaviour, not to establish any fusion claim.

disjoint from validation), EfficientNet-B6, seven transforms, and
\emph{no bootstrap intervals} --- point estimates only, at a sample size an
order of magnitude below the confirmatory analyses. Results are in

\section{Limitations}

We distinguish limitations we removed by experiment from those that remain,
because the two have different consequences for a reader.

\textbf{Removed: the control matched only energy.} This was the deepest
limitation of the earlier design and it is now addressed by construction
(\S\ref{sec:spectral}--\ref{sec:control-hierarchy}) rather than by caveat. The
residual limitation is narrower: spectrum matching is second-order, so a
watermark could still differ from the control in higher-order or phase
structure that our arm randomises away. What we can say is that the apparent
effect does not survive matching on energy \emph{and} spectrum.

\textbf{Removed: one assumed cost triple.} Replaced by a $63$-cell sweep
(\S\ref{sec:calibration}), which reversed the conclusion drawn from the single
cell. The costs are still assumed rather than elicited from a deployment; what
the sweep buys is that the conclusion no longer depends on which cell we
happened to choose.

\textbf{Reduced: the mechanism was unresolved.} Input resolution is now tested
and excluded (\S\ref{sec:mechanism}). Architecture, training data and training
augmentation remain untested, so the mechanism is narrowed to
``frequency-dependent attenuation specific to this checkpoint'' rather than
identified. \S\ref{sec:frequency} also shows frequency band alone does not
account for the watermark's own position, so even the frequency account is
partial.

\textbf{Reduced: a two-detector panel.} \S\ref{sec:control-hierarchy} widens
the panel to four detectors across three architecture families, which is
enough to show the phenomenon is architecture-dependent rather than universal
or absent. It is not enough to say which population of detectors exhibits
which pattern, or why: three architecture families is a small sample of
architecture space, all four detectors share one corpus and two watermarking
schemes, and \S\ref{sec:e0} shows floor-clearing public detectors are scarce
(five of six screened checkpoints operate at chance on our distribution), so
the three added probes are self-trained rather than drawn from a wider public
population. \textbf{New: the mechanism behind the fracture is unresolved.}
\S\ref{sec:discussion-architecture} names two untested hypotheses (kernel-size
sensitivity to local phase structure; attention-level payload disruption) and
we have run neither the kernel ablation nor the attention analysis that would
distinguish them, so the architecture-dependence itself joins the list of
narrowed-but-not-identified mechanisms alongside \S\ref{sec:frequency}'s
frequency account.

\textbf{Remains: the strong detector's provenance.} EfficientNet-B6's training
data is undisclosed, so contamination with our evaluation distribution cannot
be excluded. We mitigate with within-model, within-image deltas, under which
common-mode memorisation cancels to first order. Resolving it requires
information only the checkpoint's authors hold.

\textbf{Remains: the $\beta_4$ outlier lacks an interval.} The
resize-to-$0.5\times$ cell of the exploratory transform analysis
(\S\ref{sec:e4e5}) is a point estimate at $n{=}280$. We did not rerun it with
bootstrap intervals, so it stays flagged and unclaimed.

\textbf{Remains: synthesis type and dependence structure.} The corpus is
whole-image GAN synthesis against real photographs, not face-swap or
reenactment manipulation~\cite{rossler2019faceforensics}, and it carries no
identity metadata, so intervals are image-level bootstraps and no
identity-clustered resampling is possible. Neither is fixable by analysis.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{The control is the experiment.} The substantive finding here is not a
number about watermarking. It is a fact about how this class of experiment is
built. Across \S\ref{sec:control-hierarchy} every arm, detector and estimator
was held fixed. The only thing that changed was what the control was matched
on, and the conclusion inverted. A PSNR-matched control is the obvious choice.
It is what we chose, and it is not sufficient, because fixing energy leaves the
perturbation free to occupy the opposite end of the spectrum. In our case it
did.

\textbf{Architecture, not just control quality, determines what a fixed
comparison finds.}
\label{sec:discussion-architecture}
\S\ref{sec:control-hierarchy}'s four-detector panel forecloses the reading
that ``an adequate control makes the effect go away'' as a general statement:
it does on EfficientNet-B6, does not on ConvNeXt-Base, and produces a
sign-reversed effect on ViT-B/16 that spectrum matching only partially closes.
We can describe the pattern without explaining it. Two hypotheses are
consistent with the pattern and neither is tested here, so we name them as
open questions rather than findings. First, ConvNeXt's large-kernel
depthwise convolutions could be sensitive to localised spatial phase
structure that a radially-averaged spectral match does not constrain --- our
spectrum-matched control fixes the magnitude of the 2-D Fourier transform and
randomises phase globally, which need not preserve whatever local phase
coherence a depthwise kernel responds to. Second, ViT-B/16's patch
tokenisation and global self-attention process an image differently in kind
from any convolution, and the sign reversal --- the watermark attenuating
evidence \emph{more} than an energy- and spectrum-matched control, not less
--- is consistent with the payload perturbing patch-level or attention-level
structure that pure noise does not, but we have no attention-map analysis, no
patch-level ablation, and no experiment that isolates tokenisation or
attention as the cause rather than correlates with it. Both explanations also
compete with the confound noted in \S\ref{sec:setup}: EfficientNet-B6 is the
only fine-tuned, task-specific detector in the panel, so ``architecture''
and ``training regime'' are not independently varied between it and the three
probes, only among the three probes themselves. Resolving any of this needs
an experiment that holds architecture fixed and varies training regime, or
vice versa, and holds a kernel-size or attention ablation fixed against the
same controls; we have run neither and report the fracture as a result in
search of a mechanism, not a mechanism.

\textbf{What a null does and does not mean.} We do not detect
\cite{wu2024watermarksbugs}'s effect once the control is adequate, which is a
boundary condition rather than a refutation. Their detectors are trained for
the manipulation family they evaluate and their images are manipulated
\emph{after} marking, so watermark and forgery signals coexist in the way
their overlap argument describes. Ours are zero-shot detectors on whole-image
synthesis marked after the fact. What our result does imply for that
literature is procedural: a reported interference effect measured against an
energy-matched control cannot rule out spectral confounding unless the
relevant residual spectra are examined.

\textbf{Our own gate had the asymmetry it warns about.} We gated on
$\Delta\mathrm{AUC}_{net}$ while the quantity a calibration-centred thesis
needs is $\Delta\mu_{net}$; every reading of the latter is post-hoc with
respect to that gate, which is why it was re-registered before being tested
rather than reinterpreted in place. The same discipline is why
\S\ref{sec:calibration}'s cost sweep is labelled secondary: it reuses data the
confirmatory test already used.

\textbf{One checkpoint is not a detector, and one cell is not a cost model.}
Three of this paper's corrections came from widening a design that had
collapsed to a single point. One checkpoint became two, and a generality
claim reversed. Two became four, and a second claim -- that an adequate
control closes the gap -- narrowed from general to checkpoint-specific in the
same widening. One cost triple became sixty-three cells, and a decision-risk
null reversed. None of the three widenings was expensive. The pattern is
worth more than any single result: every generality claim in this paper that
was checked against a wider design either reversed or narrowed, and none
survived widening unchanged.

\section{Conclusion}

We set out to measure whether provenance watermarking interferes with passive
synthetic-image detection. Against a payload-free control matched per image to
the watermark's PSNR, the answer looked clear: on $20{,}000$ held-out images an
EfficientNet-B6 detector's evidence is attenuated $\approx16\%$ under the
control and left near identity under watermarking, so the degradation is not
attributable to the watermark.

That conclusion does not survive a better control, on this checkpoint. The
PSNR-matched arm is spectrally inverted relative to the watermark it stands in
for --- both tested watermarks place $0.83$--$0.97$ of their residual power in
the lowest radial band, their controls place $0.46$ in the highest --- and a
payload-free arm carrying the watermark's own spectrum closes the gap from
$+0.126$ [$+0.105,+0.146$] to $+0.006$ [$-0.009,+0.021$] on EfficientNet-B6.
With the arms, detector and estimator unchanged, matching the control's
spectrum removes the detectable watermark--control slope difference on that
checkpoint. The remaining gap is not distinguishable from zero there.

It does not close the same way everywhere. Scoring the identical $800$-image
slice through two more disclosed-provenance probes --- ConvNeXt-Base and
ViT-B/16, frozen backbones with linear heads, trained the same way as the
ResNet-18 probe --- shows the closure is architecture-dependent. ConvNeXt-Base
keeps a small, statistically reliable, same-signed gap against the
spectrum-matched control on both watermarking schemes ($+0.039$, $+0.033$,
both excluding zero). ViT-B/16 reverses which arm attenuates more even before
spectrum matching ($-0.153$, $-0.239$ against the PSNR-matched null, the
opposite sign from EfficientNet-B6), and spectrum matching narrows but does
not fully close that gap either. We name two untested hypotheses for the
fracture --- kernel-size sensitivity to local phase structure the
spectrum-matched control does not constrain, and payload disruption of
attention-level structure a frozen linear head cannot itself explain --- and
report neither as established, because EfficientNet-B6 is fine-tuned on an
undisclosed corpus while the other three are frozen probes on ours, so
architecture and training regime are not independently varied across the full
panel. \textbf{The corrected claim is not ``the effect disappears with an
adequate control.'' It is ``whether the effect survives an adequate control
depends on the detector, and we can report the pattern without yet explaining
it.''}

On EfficientNet-B6 specifically, the residual phenomenon is narrower and
belongs to a model rather than to detection generally: attenuation grows
monotonically with the perturbation's frequency band at fixed PSNR
($0.908/0.868/0.809$ for low/mid/high), the ResNet-18 probe matched to within
$0.003$ baseline AUC does not show it in a full-scale replication, and a
resolution sweep from $224$ to $448$ shows that input resolution does not
explain it over that range on that pair, while leaving architecture, training
data and augmentation untested. Three supporting measurements accompany this:
both tested watermarks keep a readable payload through $25\%$ manipulated area
across four manipulation families ($9{,}600$ measurements), so the provenance
feature is constant over the range a face replacement occupies; attenuation
degrades isotonic calibration, and over a $63$-cell cost grid the decision-risk
null we previously reported does not hold; and five of six screened public
checkpoints operate at chance on our distribution.

Two results are analytic and independent of all of this: a mean-shift statistic
cannot distinguish attenuation from translation, and a strictly increasing
rescaling leaves AUC exactly unchanged, so a ranking-based gate cannot
register attenuation at all.

None of this shows that watermark--detector interference is absent in general,
and none of it shows that robust watermarks cannot evidence tampering. What it
shows is that an interference study's conclusion can be determined by a
property of its control that the matching criterion never constrained, and
that the standard criterion does not constrain it.
